Electric hospital bed price in Pakistan: functions and home use

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

The electric hospital bed price in Pakistan depends on how many powered movements it has, its height range and safe working load, the side rails and mattress included, the brand and the exchange rate. An electric hospital bed lets the user or a carer raise the backrest, lift the knees and change the bed height with a handset instead of a crank. This guide is for families and clinics comparing beds. It explains what the functions mean, which safety standard to look for, and what to check before using one at home. It does not list prices, because they change with the model and the exchange rate.

What sets the price of an electric hospital bed in Pakistan?

The price of an electric hospital bed depends mostly on how many movements it has, its height range and weight rating, the side rails and mattress included, the brand, and the import cost on the day you order.

  • Number of functions: each extra powered movement adds parts and another button on the handset.
  • Height range: a wide range lets the bed go low for getting in and out and high for carers to work.
  • Side rails: integral rails made for the bed, full-length or split, are part of the safety design.
  • Mattress: a foam mattress, an alternating air mattress or no mattress at all changes the total.
  • Frame build: safe working load, castors with brakes, and whether the bed comes apart for delivery.
  • Brand, freight and exchange rate: beds are large and heavy to ship, and the rupee rate on the order date matters.

What does an electric hospital bed do?

An electric hospital bed uses built-in motors and a hand control to change the bed's height and the shape of its sleeping surface. That is how the US rules (21 CFR 880.5100) describe an AC-powered adjustable hospital bed: a bed with a built-in electric motor and remote controls that the patient can operate to adjust height and surface contour, with movable and latchable side rails. The US Food and Drug Administration places it in Class II.

What do the functions on an electric bed mean?

A "function" is one movement the bed can make under power, so a three-function bed has three powered movements. Sellers do not always mean the same three, so ask which movements are included. The common ones are shown below.

MovementWhat it doesWhy it matters at home
BackrestRaises the head and upper bodySitting up to eat, read or talk. Some beds, such as the Invacare SB755, move the backrest in a way designed to reduce shear and friction.
Knee or leg sectionBends the mattress platform under the knees, or lowers the leg endHelps stop the user sliding down the bed. The SB755 leg section can be lowered below horizontal.
HeightRaises and lowers the whole platformA low height for getting in and out, and a higher height for carers. The Invacare Medley Ergo lists ranges of 33 to 73 cm or 40 to 80 cm, depending on how it is set up.
Whole-bed tiltTilts the whole platform head-down or head-upOffered on some beds. Ask whether the bed has it, whether it is powered and whether it can be locked.

A bed with fewer powered movements can still suit a user who mainly needs to sit up. The person's needs, decided with their doctor or physiotherapist, should set how many functions you pay for.

Which safety standard should an electric hospital bed meet?

Look for a bed that states it meets the international standard for medical beds, which is IEC 80601-2-52:2026, or its earlier version, IEC 60601-2-52. The 2026 edition replaced IEC 60601-2-52:2009 with Amendment 1:2015, so many beds on sale still quote the older number.

The standard covers basic safety and essential performance of electric and manual medical beds for adults, with or without adjustable functions. Beds for children and adults outside its size definitions fall under IEC 80601-2-89, specialty mattresses under the ISO 20342 series, and stretchers and examination tables are excluded. As an example of how makers state it, the SB755 page says the bed meets the strength demands of IEC 60601-2-52. Ask the supplier for the document that shows the claim, not just a sticker.

What is bed rail entrapment and how do you reduce the risk?

Entrapment is when a person gets caught in a gap in or around the side rails, mattress or bed frame, and it can cause serious injury or death. The US Food and Drug Administration's 2006 guidance counted about 691 reports between 1985 and 2006, of which 413 people died. Most at risk are older people who are frail, confused or restless. Reports came from hospitals, nursing homes and private homes.

The FDA guidance names seven zones where entrapment can happen and gives recommended gap limits for the first four:

  • Zone 1, within the rail: openings less than 120 mm.
  • Zone 2, under the rail between supports: less than 120 mm.
  • Zone 3, between the rail and the mattress: less than 120 mm.
  • Zone 4, under the rail at its ends: less than 60 mm.

Zones 5 to 7 are between split rails, between the rail end and the head or foot board, and between the head or foot board and the mattress end. The FDA's question-and-answer page says the bed should be checked again when the mattress becomes softer, is replaced, or an overlay is added, because these change the gaps.

Is an electric hospital bed safe to use at home?

It can be, if the bed, rails and mattress are matched and the person's needs are assessed first. The UK Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) says most bed rail incidents it has seen happened in community settings, especially nursing homes and patients' own homes.

  • Use the maker's own rails. MHRA says integral rails have been involved in far fewer incidents than third-party rails, and that rails made for a profiling bed are preferable.
  • Match the mattress. MHRA advises checking that the bed, mattress and rails together do not leave gaps that the standards do not allow.
  • Think about the handset. MHRA describes a case where a confused person raised the bed to full height and then climbed over the rail. Ask whether the handset can lock functions.
  • Consider a low bed for users at risk of falling. MHRA mentions ultra low height beds that minimise the risk of fall injuries.
  • Review the set-up whenever the user's condition changes or any part is replaced.

Rails are not right for everyone. Follow your doctor's advice and the bed maker's instructions for use.

Which mattress should go with an electric bed?

Use a mattress of the size and type the bed maker specifies, so the rails still close the gaps. A thick or very soft mattress can reduce the rail height above the mattress or open a gap at the sides. If the user is at risk of pressure sores, an alternating air mattress may be advised; see our guides to air mattresses for bed sores and bed sore prevention equipment.

Should you buy or rent an electric hospital bed?

Buying suits long-term use, while renting can suit a short recovery. Our guide on whether to buy or rent a hospital bed for home covers the trade-offs.

What should you ask the supplier before ordering?

  1. The brand and model, and which powered movements it has.
  2. The height range in centimetres and the safe working load in kilograms.
  3. The standard it meets, with the supporting document.
  4. Whether integral side rails and a matching mattress are included, and their sizes.
  5. Whether the handset can lock functions.
  6. The rated voltage and plug, and whether a battery backup is offered for power cuts.
  7. How it comes apart for delivery. The SB755, for example, has no part heavier than 25 kg.
  8. Warranty terms and the availability of spare handsets and motors.

Frequently asked questions

What is a 3-function electric hospital bed?

It is a bed with three powered movements, often the backrest, the knee section and the height. Sellers do not all mean the same three, so ask which movements are included.

Which standard should an electric hospital bed meet?

Look for IEC 80601-2-52:2026 or the earlier IEC 60601-2-52, the international standards for adult medical beds. Ask for the document that supports the claim.

Are bed rails safe on a hospital bed at home?

They can be when the rails are made for the bed, the mattress matches, and the user has been assessed. The FDA and MHRA both warn that gaps between rails, mattress and frame can trap a person.

Can I use a normal mattress on an electric hospital bed?

Use the mattress size and type the bed maker specifies. A mattress that is too thick, too soft or the wrong size can open gaps or reduce the height of the rails above it.
