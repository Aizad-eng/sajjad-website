GP clinic equipment list: what a general practice needs in Pakistan

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

A GP clinic equipment list covers examination tools (thermometer, stethoscope, validated BP monitor, weighing scale, measuring tape), point-of-care tests (glucometer, urine strips), an emergency kit (oxygen, pocket mask, AED) and sharps and waste containers. The World Health Organization (WHO) PEN package is a good base. Requirements vary by province and regulator.

Opening a general practice clinic means buying a lot of small items at once, and it is easy to forget the ones you only need in an emergency. This list is built from published guidance: the World Health Organization (WHO) package of essential noncommunicable disease interventions (WHO PEN), the Resuscitation Council UK equipment lists for primary care, and the clinic standards of the Punjab Healthcare Commission. Treat it as a starting point. What your clinic must have depends on your province, your regulator and the services you offer.

What equipment does a basic GP clinic need?

A basic GP clinic needs simple examination tools and a few point-of-care tests, and the WHO PEN implementation tools (2013) give a short core list for primary care. The table below shows that core list. It is written for low-resource primary care, so it is a minimum rather than a full fit-out.

WHO PEN also lists tools that are not machines: WHO/ISH risk prediction charts, evidence-based clinical protocols, flow charts with referral criteria, patient clinical records, a medical information register and audit tools. Budget for printing and filing as well as equipment.

Which items should a GP clinic add when the budget allows?

WHO PEN names a second group to add when resources permit, and it is a useful upgrade path for a growing clinic. That group is:

  • Nebulizer
  • Pulse oximeter
  • Blood cholesterol assay and lipid profile
  • Serum creatinine assay
  • Troponin test strips
  • Urine microalbuminuria test strips
  • Tuning fork
  • Electrocardiograph, which WHO lists only where training to read and interpret ECGs is available
  • Defibrillator

The ECG condition matters. A machine nobody can interpret is wasted money, so plan training or reporting support before you buy one. Our guide to ECG machine channels explains the main options.

What emergency equipment should a GP clinic keep?

Every GP clinic should keep a basic resuscitation kit, and the Resuscitation Council UK publishes a minimum list for primary care (last updated May 2020). Its minimum items for all primary care settings are:

  • Protective equipment: gloves, aprons and eye protection
  • Adult pocket mask with an oxygen port
  • Oxygen cylinder, with a key where needed, and oxygen tubing
  • Automated external defibrillator (AED) with adhesive pads, plus a spare set of pads
  • Razor, stethoscope and an absorbent towel

For clinics that see higher-risk patients, the same list adds portable suction with a Yankauer sucker and soft suction catheters, oropharyngeal airways in sizes 0 to 4, adult and child self-inflating bags with reservoirs, clear face masks in sizes 0 to 4, ECG electrodes, IV cannulas with dressings and tourniquets, infusion sets, syringes and needles, a sharps box, scissors and a glucose monitor. It is a UK list, so use it as a reference and follow your own clinical policies and training.

Which Pakistani standards apply to a GP clinic?

In Punjab, the Punjab Healthcare Commission publishes Minimum Service Delivery Standards (MSDS) for general practitioner, family physician and specialist clinics. At the time of writing (November 2026), its download page describes this set as 18 standards with 47 indicators. The documents are requested through a form on the Commission's website rather than downloaded directly.

If your clinic is in another province or in Islamabad, ask your provincial health department or healthcare regulator which standards apply. Requirements change, so check the current version before you finalise your equipment list.

If you plan to add X-ray, note that the Pakistan Nuclear Regulatory Authority (PNRA) regulates radiation facilities under the PNRA Ordinance 2001, and its framework requires radiation facilities and activities to be licensed or authorized by PNRA. Speak to PNRA before you order any X-ray unit.

What should you check before buying a BP monitor, glucometer or oximeter?

Check that the device has been tested against a recognised accuracy standard and that consumables will stay available. WHO PEN specifically prefers a validated blood pressure device with a digital reading where non-physician health workers take readings. Our guide to BP monitor validation explains what validation means and how to look a model up. For glucometers, confirm that strips for the exact meter model can be reordered locally. For all three, ask for the user manual and the warranty terms before you order. You can compare models on our BP apparatus page.

How should a GP clinic handle sharps and clinical waste?

A GP clinic needs separate containers for sharps, infectious waste and general waste so waste can be separated where it is produced. WHO says about 85% of health-care waste is general, non-hazardous waste and about 15% is hazardous, which may be infectious, chemical or radioactive. WHO groups hazardous waste into categories that include sharps, infectious, pathological, chemical, pharmaceutical and cytotoxic, and radioactive waste.

In practice this means puncture-resistant sharps boxes at each point of use, colour-coded bins for infectious and general waste, and an arrangement with a waste handler. Check your local rules on bin colours and collection. Our surgical disposables checklist covers gloves, syringes and other items you will reorder every month.

What furniture and room items should be on the list?

Furniture is not on the WHO PEN technology list, so plan it from your own room layout and services. Most clinics list an examination couch, a privacy screen, a good examination light, a desk and chairs, lockable storage for records and medicines, and a handwashing point in each consulting room. If you run vaccinations or keep temperature-sensitive stock, plan a dedicated refrigerator with a thermometer and a log sheet. If you do dressings or minor procedures, plan instrument sets and a way to sterilise them, or buy single-use sterile sets instead.

How do you plan the order and budget?

Plan the order from the services you will offer, then split the list into must-have, add-later and consumables. A clear list also makes quotes easy to compare.

  1. List your services. General consultations, child health, chronic disease follow-up, minor procedures and so on.
  2. Match equipment to each service. Use the WHO PEN core list and the emergency list above as the minimum.
  3. Add quantities. One set per consulting room for basic tools, plus spares for items that break or get lost.
  4. List consumables separately. Strips, mouthpieces, AED pads, electrodes, gloves and sharps boxes run out first.
  5. Write the specification. Type, size, power supply, battery or mains, and any standard the item should meet.

Prices change with brand, features, the exchange rate and the quantity you order, so request a quote on the day you are ready to buy rather than relying on an old price list.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum equipment for a GP clinic?

The WHO PEN core list for primary care is a thermometer, stethoscope, blood pressure device, measuring tape, weighing machine, peak flow meter, inhaler spacers, glucometer with strips, and urine protein and ketone strips. Add an emergency kit and waste containers.

Does a GP clinic need a defibrillator?

The Resuscitation Council UK includes an automated external defibrillator (AED) in its minimum list for all primary care settings. Check what your own regulator and clinical policies require.

Does a GP clinic need an ECG machine?

WHO PEN lists an electrocardiograph as an item to add when resources permit, and only where staff are trained to read and interpret ECGs.

Which standards apply to clinics in Punjab?

The Punjab Healthcare Commission publishes Minimum Service Delivery Standards for GP, family physician and specialist clinics, described as 18 standards and 47 indicators at the time of writing. Ask the Commission for the current version.

What kind of BP monitor should a clinic buy?

WHO PEN prefers a validated digital blood pressure device where non-physician staff take readings. Look the model up on a validation listing before you order.
