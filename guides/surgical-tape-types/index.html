Surgical tape types: paper, silk, plastic and zinc oxide tape compared

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The four common surgical tape types are paper, silk-like cloth, perforated plastic and zinc oxide. Paper tape is light and gentle for light dressings, silk-like cloth is strong for tubes and catheters, plastic is clear and tears easily, and zinc oxide tape gives strong, long-lasting hold on normal skin. Order by type, width and roll length.

Surgical tape looks like a simple item, but the type you order changes how well a dressing stays on, how the skin copes with repeated changes and whether staff can tear it with gloves on. This guide explains the four tape types most clinics ask for, paper, silk, plastic and zinc oxide, using the makers' own product descriptions, and lists what to write on your order.

What are the main types of surgical tape?

The four common types are paper tape, silk-like cloth tape, perforated plastic tape and zinc oxide tape. They differ in the backing (the strip itself) and in the adhesive on one side.

In United States rules, a medical adhesive tape is described as a strip of fabric or plastic coated on one side with adhesive, and it sits in Class I, the lowest risk class (21 CFR 880.5240). The backing decides strength, stretch and breathability. The adhesive decides how firmly it sticks and how kind it is to skin.

  • Paper tape: light, breathable, gentle adhesion, easy to tear.
  • Silk-like cloth tape: strong, no stretch, for heavier securement.
  • Plastic tape: clear, perforated, tears in a straight line.
  • Zinc oxide tape: rigid or elastic cloth with a strong zinc oxide adhesive, for normal skin.

What is paper surgical tape used for?

Paper tape is a light, breathable tape used for lightweight dressings, blood draws and secondary securement of IV lines and non-critical tubes.

The best-known example is Micropore, now listed under the Solventum name, with Solventum's pages noting that the 3M-branded tapes are moving to the Solventum brand. Solventum describes Micropore 1530 as a conformable, breathable paper tape with an acrylate adhesive, not made with natural rubber latex, that can be torn by hand and holds for about 72 hours. Essity sells a similar product, Leukopor, which it calls a hypoallergenic paper tape that is porous, water-repellent and easy to tear by hand.

Paper tape suits light dressings and secondary securement. It is not the tape to choose when something heavy or under tension needs to stay put.

What is silk surgical tape and is it real silk?

Silk surgical tape is a strong cloth tape that makers describe as silk-like, so you should not assume it is made of real silk.

Solventum lists the material of Durapore 1538 as silk-like and calls it a high-strength, no-stretch tape. It tears both lengthwise and crosswise, and the maker lists uses such as securing urinary catheters, nasogastric and orogastric tubes, and patient positioning. Solventum also says it is not recommended where there is a lot of fluid or secretions, and that tube position should be monitored routinely. Essity's equivalent, Leukosilk, is described as a white, hypoallergenic cloth tape with strong adhesion that is hand-tearable and water-repellent.

What is plastic surgical tape used for?

Plastic surgical tape is a clear, perforated tape used for general securement where staff want to see the skin under the tape.

Solventum describes Transpore 1527 as a transparent, perforated plastic tape with an acrylate adhesive, not made with natural rubber latex. It tears in a straight line in both directions, which makes it easier to handle with gloves on, and the maker states good adhesion to dry skin for up to 72 hours. Listed uses include blood draws, lightweight dressings, IV lines and tubing as secondary securement, and non-critical tubes. Essity's Leukofix is another clear tape, described as hypoallergenic, hand-tearable in any direction and water-repellent.

What is zinc oxide tape and when is it used?

Zinc oxide tape is a cloth tape with a strong zinc oxide adhesive, used to hold dressings firmly on normal, non-sensitive skin.

Essity's hospital catalogue describes Leukoplast Standard as a rigid, tan zinc oxide tape with strong adhesion for long wear, a porous backing and a water-repellent finish, and says it is well tolerated by normal skin. The catalogue lists indications as securing dressings, heavy-duty and rough work, and non-sensitive skin types. Related products include Leukoplast elastic, a stretchy zinc oxide tape for areas that move, and Leukoplast Sleek, a waterproof PVC tape with a zinc oxide adhesive. A Sleek LF version is sold with a latex-free adhesive, so if latex allergy is a concern, ask for the latex status of the exact product before you order.

What widths and roll lengths does surgical tape come in?

In the catalogues we checked, surgical tape mostly comes in widths of 1.25 cm, 2.5 cm, 5 cm and 7.5 cm, on rolls of about 5 m or 9 m, with some shorter rolls.

How should surgical tape be applied and removed?

Makers advise applying tape without stretching it and removing it slowly, close to the skin.

Solventum's instructions for Micropore, Transpore and Durapore say that pulling or stretching the tape during application can cause tension blisters, and that firm pressure is needed to activate the acrylate adhesive. For removal, they say not to pull the tape straight up from the skin, to loosen the leading edge, to remove it low and slow while supporting the skin at the peel line, and to consider a medical adhesive remover for tape that is stuck strongly. For patient-specific skin problems, follow your doctor's or nurse's advice.

What should you write on a surgical tape order?

Write the tape type, brand or equivalent, width, roll length and number of rolls, so every supplier quotes the same thing.

  1. Type: paper, silk-like cloth, plastic, zinc oxide (rigid or elastic) or waterproof.
  2. Brand and code if you have a preference, for example Micropore 1530-1, or write "or equivalent".
  3. Width and length: for example 2.5 cm x 9.1 m.
  4. Quantity: number of rolls or boxes, and rolls per box.
  5. Latex status if any user has latex allergy.
  6. Dispenser if you want a cutter or spool holder.

Frequently asked questions

Which surgical tape is best for sensitive skin?

Makers describe paper tapes such as Leukopor as hypoallergenic, and Solventum describes Micropore as a gentle tape. For a specific patient's skin problem, follow your doctor's or nurse's advice.

Is silk surgical tape made of real silk?

Not necessarily. Solventum lists the material of its Durapore tape as silk-like, and Essity describes its Leukosilk as a cloth tape, so check the material on the product sheet.

Is zinc oxide tape waterproof?

Standard zinc oxide tape such as Leukoplast Standard is described as water-repellent, not waterproof. Waterproof versions, such as Leukoplast Sleek, use a PVC backing with a zinc oxide adhesive.

Does surgical tape contain latex?

It depends on the product. Micropore and Transpore are described as not made with natural rubber latex, while some zinc oxide tapes are sold in separate latex-free versions, so ask for the latex status of the exact product.

What size surgical tape should a clinic stock?

There is no fixed rule. Count the rolls of each type and width you used over the last few months and order that amount plus a margin for delivery time.
