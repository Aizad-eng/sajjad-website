Air mattress for bed sores in Pakistan: alternating pressure mattresses explained

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

An air mattress for bed sores is an alternating pressure system: a pump fills and empties rows of air cells in turn so pressure on the body keeps moving. Choose between a bubble pad overlay, a deeper cell overlay or a full mattress replacement, and check weight capacity, cell height, cycle time and alarms.

An air mattress for bed sores is one of the most common items families buy when someone is cared for in bed at home. This guide explains how alternating pressure mattresses work, the difference between a bubble pad and a cell mattress, what low air loss means, and what to check on the specification sheet. It covers the equipment only. The care plan, including how often the person is turned, should come from their doctor or nurse.

What is an alternating pressure air mattress?

An alternating pressure air mattress is a mattress or pad made of many air cells that a pump fills and empties in turn, so the pressure on any one part of the body keeps shifting. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) defines it in 21 CFR 880.5550 as a mattress with multiple air cells, filled and emptied in an alternating pattern by a control unit, to change the distribution of body pressure regularly and automatically. The FDA classes it as a Class II medical device and describes its use as preventing and treating bed sores, also called pressure ulcers.

Every system has two parts: the mattress or pad, and an electric pump (control unit) connected by air tubes.

Who uses an air mattress for bed sores?

These mattresses are used for people who spend long periods in bed and are at risk of pressure ulcers. The UK National Health Service (NHS) says pressure ulcers usually form over bony areas such as the heels, elbows, hips and tailbone, and that people with limited mobility are most at risk. The NHS lists specially designed mattresses and cushions among the ways to prevent them, alongside changing position often and checking the skin daily.

Whether a person needs an air mattress, and which type, is a decision for their doctor or nurse. Follow your doctor's advice. If a sore or red patch appears, get it checked by a health professional rather than relying on the mattress.

What is the difference between a bubble pad and a cell (tube) mattress?

A bubble pad has many small, shallow air bubbles, while a cell mattress has fewer, deeper air tubes running across the bed. The bubble pad is thinner, lighter and simpler. The Drive 14001E is a vinyl pad about 2.5 inches high with 130 cells.

Deeper cells give more air between the body and the bed base. The US Medicare policy article for powered overlays (A52489) requires inflated air cells of 2.5 inches or more for that category. It also says the cell height, spacing, cycling and air pressure must together give enough lift to prevent bottoming out. Cell mattresses often have removable cells. Drive says the cells on its 14027 can be removed and replaced one by one, which helps if a single cell is damaged.

What does low air loss mean on an air mattress?

Low air loss means the mattress lets a small, steady flow of air pass through or under the cover to help keep the skin surface cool and dry. Drive describes the feature on its Med-Aire 5 inch and 8 inch systems as microclimate control, combined with alternating pressure. These systems also tend to use a vapour-permeable cover. The 14027 has a removable, fluid-resistant nylon stretch cover described as low shear and vapour permeable. Low air loss systems need a stronger pump than a basic bubble pad, which is one reason they cost more.

What should you check before buying an air mattress?

Check the weight capacity, cell height, cycle time, pressure setting, alarms and the size of the bed. A short checklist:

  • Weight capacity: it must exceed the user's weight. The 14001E pad is rated 240 lb, the cell systems 350 lb.
  • Pressure setting: variable pressure lets the carer adjust for the user's weight. Drive sells the bubble pad in both variable (14001E) and fixed (14002E) pressure versions.
  • Alarms: a low pressure alarm warns the carer if the mattress is losing air. The 14027 has audible and visual alarms. The 14025NS lists a visual alarm.
  • Static mode: some pumps can hold all cells at the same pressure, useful during care tasks. The 14027 control unit offers this.
  • CPR valve: a quick-deflate valve lets the mattress be emptied fast in an emergency. The 14027 has one.
  • Size: measure the bed. A thicker mattress also raises the sleeping surface, so check the height of any side rails above it.
  • Power: the pump runs all the time, so plan for power cuts with a UPS or by asking the supplier how long the cells hold air when unplugged.

How do you know if an air mattress is too soft?

A simple check is to make sure the person does not bottom out, meaning their body sinks through the air cells onto the base. The US Medicare coverage policy for these surfaces (LCD L33830) describes the test. A carer places a hand, palm up, between the mattress and the person's bony areas. If they can easily feel the bone, the person has bottomed out. The policy checks this lying flat, with the head raised up to 30 degrees, and lying on the side. Adjust the pressure setting following the manual if this happens.

Does an air mattress replace turning the patient?

No, an air mattress is one part of the care plan and does not replace position changes or skin checks. The NHS lists changing position often, checking the skin daily and using special mattresses and cushions as separate steps. MedlinePlus guidance for people in bed also covers regular position changes, padding between pressure points and daily skin checks alongside the mattress. Follow the turning schedule your doctor or nurse gives you.

What changes the price of an air mattress in Pakistan?

The price depends mainly on the type, the pump and the features. A bubble pad with a basic pump is the simplest system. A deep cell mattress replacement with low air loss, alarms and a CPR valve costs more. Other factors:

  • Brand and model: branded imports cost more than generic units.
  • Cover material: vapour-permeable, fluid-resistant covers cost more than plain vinyl.
  • Spare parts: replacement pads, cells and tubes.
  • Exchange rate and freight: most systems are imported, so the rupee rate on the day matters.

Frequently asked questions

Which air mattress is best for bed sores?

No single type suits everyone, and the choice should follow the doctor's or nurse's advice. Bubble pads are the simplest option, while deeper cell mattresses with low air loss offer more air depth and features such as alarms and a CPR valve.

What is a bubble air mattress?

It is a thin vinyl pad of small air bubbles that sits on the existing mattress and is inflated by an alternating pump. Drive's Med-Aire 14001E, for example, has 130 cells about 2.5 inches high and a 5-minute cycle.

Does an air mattress need electricity all the time?

Yes. The pump runs continuously to keep the cells cycling, so plan for power cuts and check the low pressure alarm on the model you buy.

Can an air mattress go on a normal bed?

Overlays are designed to sit on top of an existing mattress, and Drive says its 5 inch overlay fits on any standard foam or innerspring mattress. Measure the bed and check side rail height before ordering.

Does an air mattress mean the patient does not need turning?

No. Health services such as the NHS list position changes, daily skin checks and special mattresses as separate parts of prevention, so follow the care plan from your doctor or nurse.
