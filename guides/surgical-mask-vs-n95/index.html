Surgical mask vs N95 respirator: what is the difference and which should you order?

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

A surgical mask is a loose-fitting barrier against splashes and large droplets, while an N95 respirator seals to the face and must filter at least 95 percent of a test aerosol. Surgical masks are rated under EN 14683 or ASTM F2100. N95s are approved by the US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) under 42 CFR Part 84.

Clinics in Pakistan often order "masks" as one line item, but a surgical mask and an N95 respirator are built and tested for different jobs. This guide explains what each one is, what the standards on the box mean (EN 14683, ASTM F2100 and NIOSH 42 CFR Part 84), how to check an N95 label and what to write on your order. It is a buying guide, not infection control advice. Your hospital's infection control policy decides which item staff wear for which task.

What is the difference between a surgical mask and an N95 respirator?

A surgical mask is a loose barrier against splashes and large droplets, while an N95 respirator is designed to seal to the face and filter small airborne particles.

The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) describes a surgical mask as a loose-fitting, disposable device whose edges are not meant to seal against the face. The FDA also notes that it does not filter or block very small particles in the air. An N95 is designed for a very close facial fit and very efficient filtration of airborne particles, with edges that form a seal around the nose and mouth.

Fit matters as much as the filter. The French occupational safety institute INRS reports that medical masks it put through a total inward leakage test all showed very high leakage, so they cannot claim to give respiratory protection. A surgical mask still has a clear role. A Halyard clinical note lists its purposes as shielding staff from splashes and sprays, and shielding patients from germs carried in the wearer's mouth or nose.

What do EN 14683 Type I, Type II and Type IIR mean?

EN 14683 is the European standard for medical face masks, and it sorts them into Type I, Type II and Type IIR by filtration, breathing resistance and splash resistance.

  • Type I: bacterial filtration efficiency (BFE) of at least 95 percent. The testing body Kiwa describes it as the lowest level, meant for relatively low-risk situations and not for use during operations.
  • Type II: BFE of at least 98 percent, but no splash test.
  • Type IIR: BFE of at least 98 percent plus splash resistance of at least 16.0 kPa. The "R" means splash resistant.

The World Health Organization (WHO) technical specification of 7 August 2020 lists EN 14683 Type IIR (fluid resistant) or Type II for health workers, and Type I as a mask for patients.

What do ASTM F2100 Level 1, Level 2 and Level 3 mean?

ASTM F2100 is the US specification for medical face mask materials, and its Levels 1 to 3 mainly differ in filtration and how much synthetic blood pressure the mask resists.

Level 1 needs at least 95 percent BFE and particulate filtration efficiency (PFE). Levels 2 and 3 need at least 98 percent. The splash test fires synthetic blood at 80, 120 or 160 mm Hg for Levels 1, 2 and 3. BFE uses bacteria-laden aerosol around 3 microns and PFE uses particles around 0.1 micron, according to a Halyard technical sheet. That sheet also notes that the standard requires the level to be shown on the packaging.

What makes a respirator an N95 under NIOSH 42 CFR Part 84?

A respirator is an N95 only when the US National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health (NIOSH) has approved it under 42 CFR Part 84, which requires at least 95 percent filter efficiency.

The regulation (section 84.170) sets three series. N-series filters are for workplaces free of oil aerosols, while R and P series are for particles that include oil. Each series has three efficiency classes: 95, 99 and 100, where 100 means at least 99.97 percent. Under section 84.174, N-series filters are tested with a sodium chloride aerosol with a count median diameter of about 0.075 micrometres, at 85 litres per minute, after being stored for 25 hours at high humidity. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) explains that this is a near worst-case aerosol, and an N95 must let no more than 5 percent of it through.

What is a surgical N95 and how is it different?

A surgical N95 is an N95 that is also cleared by the FDA as a medical device, so it is tested for fluid resistance as well as filtration.

The FDA calls surgical N95s a subset of N95 respirators used in health care. US rule 21 CFR 878.4040 says these respirators must be NIOSH approved, must show biocompatibility for parts that touch the user, and must be tested for flammability and resistance to fluids such as blood. Most other N95s are made for industrial work, such as construction. The WHO specification lists a NIOSH approved surgical N95, or an FFP2 that also meets EN 14683 Type IIR, as examples of fluid-resistant respirators. For more on international ratings, see our guide to KN95 vs N95 vs FFP2 respirators.

Does an N95 respirator need a fit test?

Yes, tight-fitting respirators such as N95s are meant to be fit tested, because the seal decides how much air leaks around the filter.

The US workplace rule 29 CFR 1910.134 requires a fit test before first use, whenever a different size, style, model or make is used, and at least once a year. It also bars tight-fitting facepieces where facial hair comes between the seal and the face, and asks the wearer to do a user seal check each time the respirator is put on. The FDA adds that N95s are not designed for children or people with facial hair. These are US rules, so follow your own hospital's respiratory protection policy in Pakistan.

How can you check that an N95 is genuine?

Check the printed markings on the respirator and then search its approval number on the NIOSH Certified Equipment List.

The CDC says a NIOSH approved filtering facepiece should show:

  1. The approval holder's name or logo (or the private label name).
  2. "NIOSH" in capital block letters or the NIOSH logo.
  3. A TC approval number in the form TC-84A-XXXX.
  4. The filter series and efficiency, for example N95.
  5. A model or part number.

If any of these are missing, the CDC says it is not a NIOSH approved respirator. Search the TC number on the NIOSH list and check that the model matches. Our guide on verifying a medical supplier on Alibaba covers the supplier side.

Can surgical masks and N95s be reused?

Both are generally sold as single-use items.

The FDA says surgical masks are not intended to be used more than once, and that all FDA-cleared N95 respirators are labelled single-use and disposable. Plan your order quantity on that basis, and follow your facility's policy for changing masks between patients and procedures.

What should you write on a mask or N95 order?

Write the standard and level you need, plus the details that change the product and its price.

  • Item: surgical mask, N95, or surgical N95.
  • Standard and rating: for example EN 14683 Type IIR or ASTM F2100 Level 2.
  • Attachment: ear loops or ties. The CDC notes masks come with ties, elastic or ear loops.
  • For N95s: brand, model number, TC approval number and size.
  • Pieces per box and total boxes.
  • A test report for the exact model, ideally from an ISO 17025 accredited lab, which is what the WHO purchase checklist asks for.

Frequently asked questions

Is an N95 better than a surgical mask?

They do different jobs. An N95 is designed to seal to the face and filter small airborne particles, while a surgical mask is a loose barrier against splashes and droplets. Your facility's infection control policy decides which one is worn for each task.

What does Type IIR mean on a surgical mask?

Type IIR is the EN 14683 class with at least 98 percent bacterial filtration efficiency and splash resistance of at least 16.0 kPa. The R stands for splash resistant.

Which ASTM F2100 level gives the most splash protection?

Level 3, which is tested with synthetic blood at 160 mm Hg. Level 1 is tested at 80 mm Hg and Level 2 at 120 mm Hg.

How do I check if an N95 is NIOSH approved?

Look for the approval holder's name, NIOSH in capital letters, a TC-84A approval number, the N95 rating and a model number. Then search the TC number on the NIOSH Certified Equipment List and check the model matches.

Can I wear a surgical mask more than once?

The FDA says surgical masks are not intended to be used more than once, and FDA-cleared N95s are labelled single-use. Follow your facility's policy on changing masks.
