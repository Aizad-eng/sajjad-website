Foley catheter sizes and types: French size chart, balloons and materials

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Foley catheter sizes use the French (Fr) or Charriere (Ch) scale, where 1 Fr equals 1/3 mm of outer diameter, so a 12 Fr catheter is 4 mm wide. Catalogues list children's sizes from about 8 to 10 Fr and adult sizes from 12 Fr upwards. The clinician chooses the size, balloon volume, length and material.

Foley catheters are ordered by size, number of channels, balloon volume, length and material. A small mistake in any of these means the wrong box arrives. This guide explains the French sizing system, the main types of Foley catheter and what to write on an order. It describes products only. The choice of catheter for a patient is always made by the doctor or nurse.

What does the French (Fr or Ch) size on a Foley catheter mean?

The French size is the outer diameter of the catheter, where 1 Fr (also written Ch or CH) equals one third of a millimetre. To convert, divide the French number by 3, so 12 Fr is 4 mm and 18 Fr is 6 mm across.

The scale is also called the Charriere gauge after Joseph-Frederic-Benoit Charriere, a 19th-century instrument maker in Paris, so some packs print Ch instead of Fr. Both mean the same thing. The number describes the outside of the tube, not the size of the drainage channel inside it.

What Foley catheter sizes are available?

Product lists show Foley catheters in even French sizes, and the most common range quoted runs from about 10 Fr to 28 Fr. The ICRC catalogue, for example, stocks CH 8 and CH 10 for children and CH 12 to CH 20 for adults.

Many catheters also carry a coloured band on the balloon inflation arm. Wikipedia's Foley catheter article gives examples such as black for 10 Fr, white for 12 Fr and red for 18 Fr. Do not rely on colour alone. The ICRC catalogue requires size and balloon capacity to be marked on the catheter, so read the printed numbers.

What is the difference between two-way and three-way Foley catheters?

A two-way Foley catheter has one channel for draining urine and one for filling the retaining balloon, while a three-way catheter adds a third channel for irrigation. The balloon is filled with sterile water once the tip is in the bladder, which holds the catheter in place.

Three-way catheters are mainly used after bladder or prostate surgery, so that fluid can be run in to wash out blood and clots. General supply catalogues may carry only the two-way type. The ICRC catalogue, for example, lists two-way catheters only, so ask specifically if you need three-way.

What balloon volumes do Foley catheters have?

Balloon volume is set by the manufacturer and printed on the catheter, and it does not follow automatically from the French size. A 10 Fr Bard silicone catheter is listed with a 3 ml balloon, while NHS Lanarkshire lists 5 ml balloons for 12 Ch and 14 Ch, 5 to 10 ml for 16 Ch, and 10 ml for 18 Ch.

The ICRC catalogue lists 3 to 5 ml balloons for its children's sizes and 15 ml for adult sizes. At the time of writing (November 2026), the NHS Lanarkshire formulary also advises against 30 ml balloons. Order the balloon volume your clinician specifies, and check it on the pack before use.

What materials and coatings are Foley catheters made from?

Foley catheters are mainly made from silicone or from natural rubber latex with a coating. Coatings include PTFE, hydrogel and silicone elastomer, and the coating affects whether a catheter is rated for about 28 days or about 3 months in place.

  • Silicone-coated latex: the type in the ICRC catalogue, CH 8 to CH 20.
  • All-silicone: latex-free. NHS Lanarkshire lists an all-silicone catheter licensed by its maker for up to 12 weeks. Bard lists its silicone Foley as made without natural latex.
  • Silver-coated: sold with the aim of reducing infection, although evidence of benefit is described as limited.

If staff or patients have a latex allergy, write latex-free on the order. NHS advice says most indwelling catheters need changing at least every 3 months, and suprapubic catheters usually every 4 to 12 weeks, so match the product's rated duration to the clinician's plan.

What is the difference between standard and female length catheters?

Female length catheters are shorter than standard length catheters, and the NHS Lanarkshire formulary states that a standard length catheter should always be used for male patients. The formulary lists 12, 14 and 16 Ch in both lengths.

Paediatric catheters are shorter again. The ICRC catalogue gives about 30 cm for children's catheters and about 40 cm for adult catheters. Check the length in cm on the product sheet before ordering.

What tip types are available on Foley catheters?

Most Foley catheters have a straight, rounded tip with two drainage eyes, but curved and open-tip designs are also made. The ICRC specification describes a round, atraumatic tip with two opposing eyelets.

  • Straight tip: the standard design, as in the Bard 10 Fr listing.
  • Coude tip: a tip bent at about 45 degrees, designed to pass an enlarged prostate.
  • Councill tip: a small hole at the tip so the catheter can be passed over a guidewire.

Who decides which Foley catheter size to use?

The doctor or nurse who places the catheter decides the size, length, balloon volume and material for each patient. Buyers only need to stock what the clinical team asks for.

For context, the US CDC guideline suggests considering the smallest bore catheter consistent with good drainage. NHS Lanarkshire lists 12, 14 or 16 Ch for routine adult catheterisation and no smaller than 16 Ch for suprapubic use. The ICRC catalogue notes 8 to 10 for children and 16 to 18 as the most common adult sizes. These figures help a clinic plan stock. They are not a guide to treating any patient.

What should you write on a Foley catheter order?

List the size, channels, balloon, length, material and quantity, so the supplier quotes the exact catheter.

  1. Size in Fr or Ch, for example 14 Fr.
  2. Two-way or three-way.
  3. Balloon volume in ml.
  4. Length: standard, female or paediatric.
  5. Material: coated latex or all-silicone, and whether latex-free is required.
  6. Tip type: straight, Coude or other.
  7. Sterile, single-use packing and box quantity.
  8. Matching items such as urine bags, syringes and sterile water.

The ICRC catalogue names EN 1616, the European standard for sterile urethral catheters for single use, in its specification. Ask the supplier which standard the product meets, and see our guide to DRAP medical device classes for the Pakistani regulatory side.

Frequently asked questions

What does 16 Fr mean on a Foley catheter?

It means the catheter has an outer diameter of 16 French, and since 1 Fr equals 1/3 mm, that is about 5.3 mm. Ch and Fr are the same scale.

What is the difference between Fr and Ch?

There is no difference in size. Ch stands for Charriere, the Paris instrument maker who created the scale, and Fr stands for French, so 14 Ch and 14 Fr are the same diameter.

What is a three-way Foley catheter used for?

A three-way catheter has an extra channel so fluid can be run into the bladder for irrigation. It is mainly used after bladder or prostate surgery to wash out blood and clots.

Are silicone Foley catheters latex-free?

All-silicone catheters such as the Bard silicone Foley are listed as made without natural latex, while many cheaper catheters are coated latex. Write latex-free on your order if it is required.

What balloon size does a Foley catheter have?

It depends on the product and is printed on the catheter. Examples include 3 ml on a 10 Fr Bard catheter, 5 to 10 ml on NHS-listed adult silicone catheters and 15 ml on ICRC adult catheters.
