TDH39 vs DD45 vs DD65 vs DD450: audiometric headphones compared

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

The TDH39 (Telephonics) and DD45 (RadioEar) are 10 ohm supra-aural headphones covering 100 Hz to 8 kHz. The DD65v2 is a 10 ohm circumaural headset with more noise isolation. The DD450 is a 40 ohm circumaural headset for high frequency testing. Each model has its own reference values, so any swap needs the audiometer set up and calibrated for that headset.

The headphone is the part of an audiometer that patients touch every day, so cushions crack, cords break and headsets get replaced. The four names you will see most often are the TDH39, DD45, DD65 and DD450. This guide compares them using manufacturer datasheets, explains which audiometers list them, and covers the calibration step that buyers often forget.

Who makes the TDH39, DD45, DD65 and DD450?

  • TDH39 is a Telephonics headphone. A US patent describes it as a metal cased headphone that has been widely used and accepted in the audiometry industry for many years. A plastic cased version is listed as the TDH-39P.
  • DD45 is a RadioEar supra-aural earphone. RadioEar says it was engineered to match the performance of the most popular audiological earphones as a more economical alternative. The instructions for use state it is manufactured for RadioEar by DGS Diagnostics in Middelfart, Denmark.
  • DD65v2 is a RadioEar circumaural headset. RadioEar describes it as a renewal of the classic Peltor based audiometric headphones.
  • DD450 is a RadioEar circumaural headset for standard and high frequency testing.

RadioEar also sells DD45 variants: the DD45C for contralateral use and the DD45S shielded version.

The older DD45 datasheet gives a typical sensitivity of 106.7 dB SPL. The current instructions for use give 108 dB plus or minus 3 dB. Both are RadioEar documents.

What is the difference between supra-aural and circumaural headphones?

Supra-aural headphones such as the TDH39 and DD45 press on the outer ear. They are light, and the DD45 is the standard headset on the Interacoustics AD629 and AC40. Circumaural headphones such as the DD65v2 and DD450 surround the ear with a larger cup. RadioEar lists ambient noise attenuation figures for both of its circumaural models. The DD65v2 table shows 32.4 dB of attenuation at 1000 Hz and 43.6 dB at 2000 Hz, which helps in a room that is not fully sound treated. The trade off is weight and a stronger headband: 330 g and 10 N for the RadioEar circumaural models against 4.5 N for the supra-aural ones.

Which audiometers use these headphones?

Audiometer makers list the transducers they support. Interacoustics is a good reference because it publishes full specifications.

  • The AD629 diagnostic audiometer includes a DD45 headset. TDH39, DD450 and DD65v2 headsets are listed as optional.
  • The AC40 clinical audiometer includes a DD45 headset. A TDH39 with Amplivox headset and a DD450 are listed as optional.
  • The AS608 screening audiometer lists calibration for the DD65.

A university laboratory wiki lists the TDH-39P as normally used with several Madsen audiometers and the Siemens Unity2. If your audiometer is from another brand, check its manual or ask the manufacturer which headsets it supports.

Are the TDH39, DD45 and DD65 interchangeable?

Physically they often are. The TDH39, DD45 and DD65v2 are all listed as 10 ohm, and RadioEar sells DD45 and DD65v2 headsets with 6.3 mm mono jack plugs. The DD450 is 40 ohm, so it needs an audiometer that supports it.

Acoustically they are not interchangeable. Each model produces a different sound pressure for the same input, and each has its own reference values. At 1000 Hz on an IEC 60318-1 ear simulator, RadioEar lists a RETSPL of 7.5 dB SPL for the DD45, 4.5 dB for the DD65v2 and 5.5 dB for the DD450. If you plug a different headset into an audiometer set up for another model, the hearing levels on the screen will not be correct at every frequency.

Why does calibration matter when you change headphones?

Calibration ties the audiometer dial to the real sound reaching the ear. RadioEar's DD45 instructions tell users to make sure the headset is calibrated to the audiometer it will be used with before use. In practice, a service engineer selects the correct headset model in the audiometer, measures its output on an ear simulator or coupler, and adjusts the audiometer to the reference values for that model. Plan for this whenever you replace a headset, even with the same model, since individual units vary within tolerance.

When does the DD450 make sense?

The DD450 is the option for clinics that need high frequency audiometry. RadioEar rates it from 20 Hz to 20 kHz and states it complies with IEC 60645-1 and ANSI S3.6 up to 16 kHz. Its datasheet quotes Professor Robert Margolis of the University of Minnesota, who says no clinically important differences are expected between results with the DD450 and the Sennheiser HDA 200, the headphone named in ISO 389-8. Your audiometer must have a high frequency option, such as the one Interacoustics offers on the AD629, for this to be useful.

How should audiometric headphones be cleaned and maintained?

RadioEar's DD45 instructions say to clean the headphones with a soft, damp cloth, to avoid organic solvents and aromatic oils, and to clean the cushions with a lint free cloth lightly dampened in cleaning solution if needed. Moisture must not get into the speaker. The same instructions warn not to use a defective device and to connect the headset only to equipment approved to IEC 60601-1 and IEC 60601-1-2.

Cushions, cords and headbands wear out before the transducers do. RadioEar lists separate spare parts for each model: red and blue or black ear cushions, 2 m and 5 m cords and a contralateral headband for the DD45, and ear cushions, replacement headbands and four cable types for the DD65v2 and DD450. It also sells a sanitation kit designed for the DD65v2 and DD450. A worn cushion or cord can be replaced on its own, but treat any repair to the earphone itself as a reason to recheck calibration.

What should you check when buying a replacement headset?

  1. The exact model and maker on the original headset label or audiometer manual, not just the look of the cups.
  2. Impedance and plug type: 6.3 mm mono jacks, 3.5 mm mini jack, straight or angled.
  3. Whether your audiometer supports the model, and whether its calibration data for that model is installed.
  4. Who will calibrate the audiometer with the new headset, and when.
  5. Spare parts such as cushions and cords. RadioEar sells these separately for the DD45, DD65v2 and DD450.
  6. Paper proof of origin, such as the manufacturer's datasheet and packing list, so you know what you are getting.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DD45 a replacement for the TDH39?

RadioEar designed the DD45 to match the performance of the most popular audiometric earphones, and both are 10 ohm supra-aural models. They use different reference values, so the audiometer must be set up and calibrated for whichever one is connected.

What impedance is the DD45?

RadioEar lists the DD45 as a 10 ohm earphone. The DD65v2 is also 10 ohm, while the DD450 is 40 ohm.

Which headset should I choose for high frequency testing?

The RadioEar DD450 is rated to 20 kHz and complies with IEC 60645-1 up to 16 kHz. Your audiometer also needs a high frequency option.

Do circumaural headphones block more noise?

Yes, that is their purpose. RadioEar lists ambient attenuation of 32.4 dB at 1000 Hz for the DD65v2, which helps in rooms that are not fully sound treated.
