How to clean and care for a stethoscope

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

To clean a stethoscope, wipe the diaphragm, bell, tubing, headset and eartips with a 70% isopropyl alcohol wipe between patients, as 3M Littmann and ADC advise. Do not soak it, sterilise it or use hand sanitiser on it. Store it away from extreme heat, cold, oils, solvents and long periods of direct sunlight.

A good acoustic stethoscope can last for many years, but only if it is cleaned the right way and kept away from the things that damage tubing and diaphragms. This guide brings together the care instructions published by three stethoscope makers, 3M Littmann, ADC (American Diagnostic Corporation) and boso, so you can see where they agree and where they differ. Always check the instructions for your own model as well.

How often should a stethoscope be cleaned?

A stethoscope should be cleaned between every patient. 3M's Littmann care sheet says the diaphragm and bell should be wiped between every patient and ideally immediately before use, and the ADC Adscope instructions also say to clean between patients. ADC's instructions warn that cross-contamination of a stethoscope could cause patient infection.

In infection control terms, items that touch only intact skin are called noncritical items. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) gives blood pressure cuffs as an example and says low-level disinfection is generally used for such items. A stethoscope chestpiece is used on skin in the same way, which is why a quick alcohol wipe between patients is the usual routine.

What should you use to clean a stethoscope?

A 70% isopropyl alcohol wipe or swab is the cleaner all three makers accept. Littmann recommends wiping the whole surface of the diaphragm and bell with a 70% isopropyl alcohol swab, and cleaning the tubing with a soft cloth moistened with a mild soap solution as well as an alcohol swab. ADC allows 70% isopropyl alcohol solution, an alcohol wipe, or a disposable wipe with soap and water, used on the chestpiece, tubing, headset and eartips.

What should you never do when cleaning a stethoscope?

Never soak a stethoscope or put it through a sterilisation process unless the maker allows it. Littmann says not to immerse a stethoscope in any liquid and not to subject it to any sterilisation process. ADC says immersion and steam sterilisation can let debris into the tubing, which can reduce sound quality, and that low-temperature gas sterilisation is the only method that will not damage its stethoscopes.

Do not use hand sanitiser on the stethoscope either. 3M explains that hand sanitisers contain moisturisers that can make single-piece diaphragms swell and fall off. Keep solvents and oils away from the tubing, as ADC lists both as causes of damage.

How do you clean the eartips and diaphragm?

Remove them, clean them, and let every part dry fully before putting the stethoscope back together. ADC says the diaphragm, non-chill bell ring and eartips may be taken off for a thorough clean, that all parts should be dry before reassembly, and that the chestpiece itself should not be removed.

  • Wipe the inside and outside of the eartips with an alcohol wipe.
  • Wipe both faces of the diaphragm and the rim it sits in.
  • Let the parts dry in the air before refitting them.
  • Refit the eartips so they lock firmly. ADC warns never to use a stethoscope without eartips firmly locked in place.

Eartips and small parts can be a choking hazard, so ADC advises keeping them properly attached and stored, and keeping the tubing away from children.

Does the tubing need special care?

Yes, because the tubing is the part most often forgotten and the part most easily damaged. 3M says the tubing is often neglected and should be cleaned with mild soap on a soft cloth and an alcohol swab. ADC lists the things that harm tubing: long periods in direct sunlight, extreme heat or cold, and solvents or oils.

Look over the tubing regularly for cracks, stiffness or sticky patches, and replace the tubing or the stethoscope if it is damaged.

How should you store a stethoscope?

Store it somewhere clean, away from direct sunlight and extreme temperatures. ADC says not to leave a stethoscope in extreme heat or cold for long periods and not to leave it in direct sunlight for long, because sunlight can damage the tubing, diaphragm and retaining rings. In Pakistan, that means not leaving it on a car dashboard or in a parked car in summer.

If you keep it in its original box, ADC suggests wiping the moulded tray too, so a clean stethoscope is not put back onto a dirty surface.

How do you wear the headset correctly?

Wear the headset with the eartips pointing forward, towards your nose. ADC says its ear tubes are angled at 15 degrees and the eartips should point forward towards the bridge of the nose for a proper seal. boso gives the same advice, saying the eartips should point forward in the direction of the ear canal.

If the headset feels too tight or too loose, both makers describe how to adjust the spring tension by gently pulling the ear tubes apart or squeezing them together. ADC cautions that adjusting too much can weaken the spring, so make small changes.

When should you replace parts or the stethoscope?

Replace a part as soon as it is cracked, torn, loose or no longer seals, rather than on a fixed schedule. The manuals we read describe how to replace diaphragms and eartips but do not give a set replacement interval. ADC explains how to snap off the rim and fit a new diaphragm, and boso explains how to roll off the protective ring to change its diaphragm and how to unscrew and replace its eartips.

Warranty terms differ by maker. At the time of writing (November 2026), ADC states that its Adscope stethoscopes carry a lifetime warranty for the original retail purchaser, which excludes damage from misuse, and boso states a three-year manufacturer's warranty from the date of purchase shown on the invoice. Littmann advises buying only from authorised dealers because counterfeit Littmann stethoscopes are in circulation. Our guide on how to spot a fake Littmann stethoscope explains what to look for, and the Littmann Classic III vs Cardiology IV guide compares two popular models.

Does a digital stethoscope need different care?

Yes, because it contains electronics and a battery, so follow the maker's own manual for cleaning and charging. The advice in this guide comes from manuals for acoustic models. Our digital stethoscope guide covers what those models do.

Frequently asked questions

Can I clean my stethoscope with hand sanitiser?

3M Littmann does not recommend it. Hand sanitisers contain moisturisers that can make single-piece diaphragms swell and come off.

Can a stethoscope be autoclaved?

Littmann says not to put its stethoscopes through any sterilisation process, and ADC says not to steam sterilise. Follow your model's manual.

How do I clean stethoscope eartips?

Remove the eartips, wipe them with a 70% isopropyl alcohol wipe and let them dry fully. Refit them so they lock firmly before use.

Which way should stethoscope eartips point?

Forward, towards your nose. ADC and boso both say the eartips should point forward to seal properly in the ear canal.
