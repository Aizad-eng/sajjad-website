FreeStyle Libre continuous glucose monitor: how it works and what to check in Pakistan

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

The FreeStyle Libre is Abbott's continuous glucose monitor. A small sensor on the back of the upper arm measures glucose in the fluid under the skin and sends a reading every minute to a phone app or reader. Libre 2 Plus and 3 Plus sensors last 15 days. You still need a glucometer for backup checks.

The FreeStyle Libre is a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) made by Abbott. Many people in Pakistan hear about it from relatives abroad or from their doctor and want to know how it works before buying. This guide explains the system, compares the current sensor versions and lists what to check when you order one. It is an equipment guide only. Whether a CGM suits you, and what you do with the numbers, is for your doctor to decide.

What is the FreeStyle Libre?

The FreeStyle Libre is a small sensor worn on the back of the upper arm that measures glucose continuously and sends readings to a phone app or a reader. Abbott's Australian site describes the sensor as measuring glucose in interstitial fluid, the fluid around the cells under the skin, rather than in blood from a finger.

On current versions the sensor sends a reading every minute. You see the trend through the day and night instead of a single number from each finger prick, and the app shows how meals, activity and medicines line up with your glucose. Abbott's safety information says the sensor should only be applied to the back of the upper arm, because it may not work properly in other places.

Which FreeStyle Libre versions are there?

At the time of writing (November 2026), Abbott's sites describe three main sensors: FreeStyle Libre 2, FreeStyle Libre 2 Plus and FreeStyle Libre 3 Plus. The Plus sensors last a day longer and are cleared for younger children. The table summarises what Abbott publishes.

Abbott's safety page also covers an older FreeStyle Libre 14 day system, which has its own rules. Product names, features and age limits differ by country, so read the leaflet for the exact version you receive.

How do readings reach your phone or reader?

Readings travel from the sensor to a compatible smartphone app or to a FreeStyle Libre reader over Bluetooth. Abbott's US pages call the app the Libre app, while the Australian page names the FreeStyle LibreLink app and the FreeStyle Libre 2 reader, which shows that app names and options vary by market.

Abbott also says the apps only work with certain phones and operating systems. Data upload to its LibreView cloud service needs an internet connection. If you plan to use a phone, check Abbott's compatibility list for your model and region before buying sensors.

How long does a sensor last and what happens at the end?

A Libre 2 Plus or Libre 3 Plus sensor lasts 15 days, then stops by itself. Abbott's support page says the sensor automatically stops collecting glucose data and should be removed 15 days after it was started. A Libre 2 sensor lasts 14 days.

After applying a new sensor there is a 60 minute warm-up with no readings. Plan sensor changes so this hour does not fall at an awkward time, and keep the next sensor ready before the current one ends.

Do you still need a glucometer with a FreeStyle Libre?

Yes, keep a working glucometer and strips. Abbott's prescriber page says a fingerstick test is needed when alarms or readings do not match symptoms, and when the Check Blood Glucose symbol appears during the first 12 hours of a sensor. Its safety information also says to use a blood glucose meter value for treatment decisions in those cases.

A glucometer also covers you if a sensor falls off, fails early or runs out before the next box arrives. See how to choose a glucometer and our glucometer page if you need one.

Can you shower, swim or have a scan while wearing it?

You can shower and swim with the sensor on, within limits. Abbott says the sensor is water resistant in up to 1 metre (3 feet) of water and should not be underwater for longer than 30 minutes.

For scans, Abbott's US safety information says Libre 2 Plus and Libre 3 Plus sensors can stay on for a 1.5T or 3T MRI under listed conditions, with the phone and reader kept outside the scanner room. All parts of the system must be removed before diathermy, a high frequency heat treatment. Tell the radiology or physiotherapy team you are wearing a sensor, and follow their instructions and the leaflet in your box.

What can affect FreeStyle Libre readings?

Several things can affect readings or stop them for a while. These are the points Abbott lists:

  • Vitamin C: taking more than 1,000 mg a day may falsely raise Libre 2 Plus readings. For Libre 3 Plus, Abbott says up to 1,000 mg a day is fine for using readings. Ask your doctor before changing any supplement.
  • Temperature: no readings are shown when the sensor is too hot or too cold.
  • Placement: the sensor is meant for the back of the upper arm only.
  • Warm-up and errors: no readings during the first hour, during error messages, or when the screen shows LO or HI.

Abbott's labelling warns that not following the instructions may mean missing a severe low or high. Read the full leaflet in your language before first use.

What should you check when buying a FreeStyle Libre in Pakistan?

Most of the risk is in buying the wrong version or a sensor that has been badly stored. Before you pay, check:

  1. Exact version. Libre 2, Libre 2 Plus and Libre 3 Plus are different sensors. Make sure the sensor matches your reader or app.
  2. Country version. Abbott runs separate sites and labelling for different countries. Ask which market the box was made for and whether the app works with your phone in Pakistan.
  3. Expiry date and seal. Check the use-by date on each sensor box and that the packaging is sealed and undamaged.
  4. Storage on the way. Read the storage temperature on the box and ask how the sensors were kept during shipping.
  5. Quantity. Each sensor lasts 14 or 15 days, so work out how many you need per month and order ahead.
  6. Rules. Medical device import rules can change. Our guide to the DRAP Medical Devices Rules explains the framework, and you should check the current rule.

Is a CGM right for you?

That is a question for your doctor, not for a supplier. A CGM gives far more readings than finger pricks, but sensors must be replaced every 14 or 15 days as an ongoing purchase, and you still need a glucometer for backup checks. Bring the version you are considering to your next appointment and ask how your doctor wants you to use the readings.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a FreeStyle Libre sensor last?

Libre 2 Plus and Libre 3 Plus sensors last 15 days and then stop on their own. The Libre 2 sensor lasts 14 days.

Does the FreeStyle Libre need finger pricks?

Day to day readings come from the sensor, but Abbott says to do a fingerstick test when readings do not match symptoms and in some situations in the first 12 hours. Keep a glucometer at home.

Can I swim with a FreeStyle Libre sensor?

Abbott says the sensor is water resistant in up to 1 metre of water for no longer than 30 minutes.

Will the FreeStyle Libre app work on my phone in Pakistan?

Abbott says its apps only work with certain phones and operating systems, and app versions differ by country. Check the compatibility list for your phone and the sensor's country version before buying.

Is FreeStyle Libre 3 Plus better than Libre 2 Plus?

The Libre 3 Plus sensor is smaller, holds 15 days of data and has a longer alarm range. Which one suits you depends on your phone, reader and your doctor's advice.
