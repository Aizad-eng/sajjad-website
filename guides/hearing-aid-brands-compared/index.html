Hearing aid brands compared: what the big makers offer

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Most well-known hearing aid brands come from five makers: Sonova (Phonak, Unitron), Demant (Oticon, Bernafon, Philips), WS Audiology (Signia, Widex, Rexton), GN (ReSound, Beltone) and Starkey. Each sells premium families with AI noise handling and Bluetooth. The right choice depends on your hearing test, the style and a local professional who can fit that brand.

Most well-known hearing aid brands belong to a handful of large groups, and each group sells several brands. Knowing who makes what helps you compare quotes, understand why two brands can share technology, and ask the right questions about fitting and service. This guide sets out what each big maker offers, using the companies' own websites at the time of writing (November 2026). It is a buying guide, not medical advice: a hearing care professional should test your hearing and recommend the device, so follow their advice.

Who are the big hearing aid makers?

The five big makers covered here are Sonova, Demant, WS Audiology, GN and Starkey. Each one owns one or more hearing aid brands:

  • Sonova: Phonak, Unitron and Hansaton, plus Advanced Bionics cochlear implants.
  • Demant: Oticon, Bernafon and Philips-branded hearing aids.
  • WS Audiology (WSA): Signia, Widex, Rexton and Audio Service.
  • GN: ReSound and Beltone.
  • Starkey: sells hearing aids under its own name.

What does Sonova offer through Phonak, Unitron and Hansaton?

Sonova offers hearing aids through Phonak, Unitron and Hansaton, cochlear implants through Advanced Bionics, and consumer audio under the Sennheiser brand. Phonak's current range on its international site includes the Audéo EON Sphere and Audéo EON R receiver-in-canal models, the Naída L, the Sky Lumity for children and the Virto Infinio custom model. Phonak says EON models stream calls and audio from virtually any Bluetooth device using its Made For All connectivity, and connect to Auracast broadcasts in places such as theatres. For people with one ear that cannot be aided, Phonak offers the CROS EON R, which routes sound from that side to the other ear.

What does Demant offer through Oticon, Bernafon and Philips?

Demant offers Oticon, Bernafon and Philips-branded hearing aids, and it also owns the audiometer makers Interacoustics and MAICO. Its history page says it bought Bernafon in 1995 and started selling Philips-branded hearing solutions through a partnership announced in 2018.

Oticon's global site features three families. Oticon Reveal is described as the first hearing aid powered by a Dual AI system, with separate speech and context processing and up to 40 dB of wind and handling noise attenuation. Oticon Intent uses 4D Sensor technology to adjust support as listening needs change. Oticon Zeal is promoted as a discreet design, and Oticon Play SI is built for children.

What does WS Audiology offer through Signia and Widex?

WS Audiology offers Signia, Widex and Rexton, plus Audio Service, which partners with independent hearing care professionals. WSA calls Widex one of its key brands in the premium segment. It also sells Vibe, an over-the-counter brand, in markets outside the United States.

  • Signia: the IX family covers most styles. Signia lists Pure Charge&Go IX receiver-in-canal models for mild to profound loss, Silk Charge&Go IX instant-fit in-canal aids, Insio custom aids, and Motion Charge&Go behind-the-ear models up to a super-power version for profound loss. Its newest platform, Multi-adaptive Xperience (MaX), is aimed at group conversations in noise.
  • Widex: WSA says Widex uses a time-domain filter bank, unlike the frequency-domain approach used by most other makers. The Widex Allure family comes in receiver-in-canal, behind-the-ear and in-the-ear styles.
  • Rexton: WSA describes Rexton aids as built tough, impact proof and water resistant.

What does GN offer through ReSound and Beltone?

GN offers ReSound and Beltone, and it launches equivalent product families under both names. In August 2026, GN announced the ReSound Sensia family and the equivalent Beltone Illuminate family. GN says Sensia combines an AI environment classifier, a 4-microphone directional beamformer and Deep Neural Network (DNN) noise reduction. Its rechargeable microRIE, ITC and ITE styles are rated for 24 hours of battery life. GN also launched bimodal versions designed to work with Cochlear Nucleus sound processors.

GN said Sensia would reach the United States, Germany and Austria first, from 20 August 2026, with other markets following over the coming months. Check availability before you plan around it in Pakistan.

What does Starkey offer?

Starkey offers three AI families under its own brand: Omega AI+, Edge AI and G Series AI. Its site promotes Bluetooth connectivity, rechargeable options and water-resistant designs. Starkey is not part of the four groups above, and its site lists Bill Austin as owner and chairman.

Does the brand matter more than the fitting?

No, the fitting matters at least as much as the brand. Each maker uses its own fitting software, so a hearing aid must be programmed by a professional who has that brand's software and training. GN, for example, released ReSound Smart Fit 2.4 alongside Sensia, built for the NAL-NL3 prescription method. Both Widex and Signia tell buyers to choose with the help of a hearing care professional. Before you choose a brand, ask whether a clinic near you can program and adjust it.

What should you check before buying a hearing aid in Pakistan?

Check the style, the fitting support and the after-sales service, not just the brand name.

  1. Style: receiver-in-canal, behind-the-ear, in-the-ear or completely-in-canal. Our hearing aid types guide explains each one.
  2. Fitting: a local audiologist or clinic with the brand's fitting software.
  3. Power: rechargeable or disposable batteries, and how easy replacements are to buy.
  4. Exact model: confirm the family, model and version on every quote, so you compare like with like.
  5. Availability: new families reach some countries months after launch.
  6. Warranty and service: ask in writing who handles repairs and how long they take.

Frequently asked questions

Which company owns Phonak?

Phonak belongs to the Sonova group, which also owns Unitron, Hansaton and the cochlear implant maker Advanced Bionics.

Are Oticon and Philips hearing aids made by the same group?

Yes. Demant owns Oticon and Bernafon, and it brings Philips-branded hearing aids to market through a partnership announced in 2018.

Are Signia and Widex the same company?

Both brands belong to WS Audiology, along with Rexton and Audio Service. They use different sound processing approaches and sell separate product families.

Which hearing aid brand is best?

No single brand is best for everyone. The right device depends on your hearing test, the style you can manage and whether a local professional can fit and adjust it, so follow your audiologist's advice.

Can any audiologist program any hearing aid brand?

Not always. Each maker uses its own fitting software, so check that your clinic has the software and training for the brand you plan to buy.
