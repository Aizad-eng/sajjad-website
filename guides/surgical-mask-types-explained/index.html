Surgical mask types explained: Type IIR, ASTM levels, N95, KN95 and FFP2

Updated November 1, 2026 · 9 min read

Quick answer

Surgical masks and respirators are different products. Surgical masks are graded by EN 14683 (Type I, II, IIR) or ASTM F2100 (Level 1, 2, 3) for bacterial filtration, breathability and splash resistance. Respirators such as N95 (NIOSH, 42 CFR 84), KN95 (China GB 2626) and FFP2 or FFP3 (EN 149) are tight-fitting and graded for filtering airborne particles.

Mask boxes carry a lot of codes: Type IIR, Level 3, N95, KN95, FFP2. They come from different standards and measure different things. This guide explains each grade in plain terms, where each type is generally used, and what to write on an order so you receive the grade you expect.

What is the difference between a surgical mask and a respirator?

A surgical mask is loose-fitting. The US FDA notes that its edges are not designed to form a seal around the nose and mouth. It is meant to block splashes and large droplets, and the FDA states that it does not filter or block very small particles in the air.

A respirator, such as an N95, is designed to achieve a very close facial fit and very efficient filtration of airborne particles. The FDA notes that N95s are not designed for children or for people with facial hair, because the seal matters. Both surgical masks and FDA-cleared N95 respirators are labelled single-use.

So the first question when ordering is not "which grade" but "mask or respirator". Surgical masks are graded under EN 14683 or ASTM F2100. Respirators are graded under NIOSH rules, GB 2626 or EN 149.

What do Type I, Type II and Type IIR mean?

EN 14683 is the European standard for medical face masks. It sets three types based on bacterial filtration efficiency (BFE), breathability (differential pressure), splash resistance and microbial cleanliness.

EN 14683 typeBacterial filtration efficiencyDifferential pressure (Pa/cm2)Splash resistanceMicrobial cleanliness (cfu/g)
Type I95% or moreUnder 40Not required30 or less
Type II98% or moreUnder 40Not required30 or less
Type IIR98% or moreUnder 6016 kPa or more30 or less

The "R" in IIR means splash resistant. Only Type IIR is tested against a jet of synthetic blood. Lower differential pressure means the mask is easier to breathe through, which is why Type IIR, with its extra fluid barrier, is allowed a higher limit.

What are ASTM F2100 Level 1, 2 and 3 masks?

ASTM F2100 is the US specification for materials used in medical face masks. It covers bacterial filtration efficiency, sub-micron particle filtration efficiency (PFE), differential pressure, resistance to synthetic blood and flammability. BFE is tested with bacteria-laden droplets of about 3 microns, and PFE with particles of about 0.1 micron.

ASTM F2100 levelFluid resistance (synthetic blood)BFE and PFE
Level 180 mmHg95% or more
Level 2120 mmHg98% or more
Level 3160 mmHg98% or more

The three pressures are meant to simulate splashes at low, normal and high blood pressure. Breathability limits also apply to each level, and the exact figures have changed between revisions of the standard, so ask which revision a test report uses. ASTM F2100 requires the performance level to be shown on the packaging.

What is an N95 respirator?

N95 is a US class of particulate respirator approved by NIOSH under 42 CFR Part 84. The number is the minimum filter efficiency and the letter describes resistance to oil aerosols:

  • N: not resistant to oil. N-series filters are restricted to workplaces free of oil aerosols.
  • R and P: oil resistant and oil proof, for particles that include oil-based liquids.
  • 95, 99 and 100: minimum filter efficiency of 95, 99 and 99.97 percent.

That gives nine classes, from N95 to P100. In healthcare, the FDA describes "surgical N95" respirators as a subset of N95s that are class II medical devices, used to protect both the patient and healthcare staff. Most N95s are regulated by NIOSH's National Personal Protective Technology Laboratory.

What is a KN95 and how does it compare?

KN95 is a grade under China's GB 2626 standard, which also sets a filtration requirement of 95 percent or more. Genuine KN95 masks are expected to be stamped with the manufacturer's name, the model and the standard, for example GB2626-2019. KN95 masks are not NIOSH-approved and usually use ear loops rather than head straps.

During the COVID-19 response, NIOSH tested many respirators from outside the US that claimed international standards, including KN95 products. Results varied widely between and within manufacturers, with some units far below 95 percent. NIOSH stated that these assessments are not part of its approval process and that no conclusions can be drawn about equivalence to approved N95s. It also noted limited testing suggested fit difficulties with ear loop designs. For a buyer, the lesson is to rely on test reports and traceable manufacturers, not on the print on the mask alone.

What do FFP2 and FFP3 mean?

FFP1, FFP2 and FFP3 are classes of filtering half masks under the European standard EN 149. The class depends on total inward leakage, filter penetration and breathing resistance.

EN 149 classTotal inward leakage (average)Maximum filter penetration (sodium chloride and paraffin oil, 95 l/min)
FFP122% or less20%
FFP28% or less6%
FFP32% or less1%

FFP3 is the highest of the three classes. FFP masks are sold in cup and flat-fold shapes, with or without an exhalation valve.

Which mask type is used where?

The choice in a facility is set by its own infection control policy and local health authority guidance, not by the supplier. As a general description of what each product is designed for:

  • Type I or Level 1 masks: lower-risk settings where splash protection is not needed.
  • Type II masks: higher bacterial filtration, still without a splash requirement.
  • Type IIR or Level 2 and 3 masks: fluid-resistant masks for procedures where splashes or sprays of blood or body fluids can occur.
  • N95, KN95, FFP2 and FFP3 respirators: tight-fitting products designed to filter airborne particles, which only work as intended with a proper fit.

How do you spot a fake or mislabelled mask?

  • N95: look for the company name, model number and the word NIOSH on the respirator. A public health guide warns that an N95 with ear loops is probably fake, because most N95s use two head straps.
  • KN95: look for the stamped manufacturer, model and GB 2626 reference. A study of respirators bought by healthcare facilities found that checking by eye did not reliably reveal counterfeit or defective products.
  • Surgical masks: the box should state the EN 14683 type or ASTM F2100 level, and the supplier should be able to share the matching test report.

What should you specify when ordering masks?

  1. Product: surgical mask, procedure mask or respirator.
  2. Grade: EN 14683 Type I, II or IIR, ASTM F2100 Level 1, 2 or 3, or respirator class (N95, KN95, FFP2, FFP3).
  3. Fastening: ear loops or ties for masks, head straps or ear loops for respirators.
  4. Valve: with or without exhalation valve for respirators.
  5. Extras: anti-fog strip or eye shield if needed.
  6. Quantity and pack size, for example masks per box and boxes per carton.
  7. Documents: test report for the stated standard and the manufacturer's details.

Frequently asked questions

Is a Type IIR mask the same as an N95?

No. A Type IIR mask is a loose-fitting surgical mask tested for bacterial filtration and splash resistance under EN 14683. An N95 is a tight-fitting respirator approved by NIOSH for filtering airborne particles.

What does the R in Type IIR stand for?

It means splash resistant. Type IIR masks must resist synthetic blood at 16 kPa or more, which Type I and Type II masks are not tested for.

Is KN95 equal to N95?

Both target 95 percent filtration, but KN95 follows China's GB 2626 standard and is not NIOSH-approved. NIOSH testing of imported KN95 products found wide variation, so ask for test reports from a traceable maker.

What is the difference between FFP2 and FFP3?

Under EN 149, FFP3 allows lower total inward leakage (2% average) and lower filter penetration (1%) than FFP2 (8% and 6%). FFP3 is the higher protection class.

Which ASTM level is the highest?

Level 3 is the highest ASTM F2100 level. It requires fluid resistance at 160 mmHg and 98 percent or more bacterial and particle filtration efficiency.
