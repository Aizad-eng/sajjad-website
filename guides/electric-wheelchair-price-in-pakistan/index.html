Electric wheelchair price in Pakistan: batteries, range and weight

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The electric wheelchair price in Pakistan depends on the battery type and size, the range, the chair's weight and folding design, the brand, and the exchange rate on the day you order. Compare the battery's watt-hour rating, the range for the user's weight and the weight with the battery fitted. SurgiSource quotes today's price in rupees through its quote form.

Electric wheelchairs, also called power chairs, range from heavy outdoor chairs to light folding models built for travel. This guide explains what moves the price in Pakistan, using figures from manufacturer specification pages and the IATA guidance on battery-powered mobility aids. It does not list prices, because they change with the exchange rate and the exact model on the day you order.

What sets the price of an electric wheelchair in Pakistan?

The price is set mainly by the battery, the frame design, the brand and the cost of importing the chair on the day you order. When a chair is imported on order, a change in the rupee rate moves the final figure even when the model stays the same.

  • Battery type and size: a bigger battery gives more range but adds weight and usually adds to the price.
  • Frame and folding design: folding travel chairs are built to be lifted into a car boot, while fixed-frame chairs are made for daily use at home and outdoors.
  • Range, speed and motors: stronger motors and longer range need more battery capacity.
  • Brand and model: branded chairs come with published specifications and user manuals. Unbranded chairs vary more, so check every figure.
  • Exchange rate, freight and duties: these change from week to week. Check the current rule for duties rather than relying on an old figure.
  • Extras: a spare battery, an extra charger, a pressure cushion or a different joystick all add to the total.

Is an electric wheelchair a medical device?

Yes, it falls within the medical device definitions used in the United States and by Pakistan's regulator. United States rules (21 CFR 890.3860) define a powered wheelchair as a battery-operated device with wheels, intended for medical purposes, that gives mobility to people restricted to a sitting position, and place it in Class II. The WHILL Model F, for example, is listed by its maker as cleared by the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as a Class II device.

In Pakistan, the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) defines a medical device to include any apparatus meant for the alleviation of, or compensation for, an injury, or for support of the anatomy. Whether a particular imported chair needs DRAP registration depends on how it is classified, so check the current rule. Our guide to the DRAP Medical Devices Rules explains the basics.

Which batteries do electric wheelchairs use?

Electric wheelchairs run on lithium-ion batteries or on lead-acid batteries. The IATA guidance on battery-powered mobility aids splits them into three groups: lithium-ion, non-spillable lead-acid (absorbed glass mat or gel types) and spillable wet batteries.

For lithium-ion batteries, the number that matters is the watt-hour (Wh) rating. IATA says lithium-ion batteries must be marked with it, and that you can work it out by multiplying the nominal voltage by the capacity in ampere-hours (Ah). If only milliampere-hours are printed, divide by 1,000 first. As a worked example, a 24 V battery rated at 10 Ah holds 240 Wh.

Ask the supplier for a photo of the battery label before you order. The Pride Jazzy Passport, for instance, is offered with a 10.4 Ah or an 18 Ah lithium-ion battery, and the larger pack gives more range. The WHILL Model F battery weighs 2.7 kg and its maker gives a charging time of up to 5 hours.

How far will an electric wheelchair go on one charge?

Treat the range on a listing as a best case, because the real distance falls as the load on the chair rises. Pride Mobility shows this clearly on its Jazzy Passport page: the chair is rated for up to 11 miles (about 17.7 km) depending on the battery package, but the same page gives up to 8.6 miles (about 13.8 km) for a 100 kg user and up to 7.9 miles (about 12.7 km) for a 113 kg user.

WHILL rates the Model F at up to 20 km on a full charge, with a top speed of 6 km/h and a maximum incline of 10 degrees. Slopes and rough roads are likely to cut into these numbers too. Ask the supplier which battery and which user weight the range figure is based on, and plan daily trips with a margin.

Can you take an electric wheelchair on a flight?

Usually yes, but the airline must approve the chair in advance and lithium batteries have watt-hour limits. The IATA guidance document sets out these points:

  • Lithium batteries must be of a type that passes the tests in the UN Manual of Tests and Criteria, Part III, subsection 38.3.
  • If the chair is designed so the user can remove the battery, the battery must not exceed 300 Wh. Where the chair needs two batteries to run, each must not exceed 160 Wh.
  • If the battery stays fitted in the chair, there is no Wh limit for it.
  • You may carry one spare battery of up to 300 Wh, or two spares of up to 160 Wh each.
  • Removed and spare batteries travel in the cabin and must be protected from damage, for example in a protective pouch.
  • Passengers should ask the airline for approval at least 48 hours before the flight, or as soon as possible.

The airline will ask for the maker, the model, the battery type and the number and Wh rating of the batteries. The IATA document we used is based on the 2019 regulations, so check the current rule with your airline before you book.

How do you choose the right size and type?

Choose the chair at a proper assessment rather than from a photo. The World Health Organization (WHO) Wheelchair provision guidelines (2023) say the best outcomes come when users get individual assessment, fitting, training and follow-up from trained personnel.

  • Seat width: the WHILL Model F, for example, has an adjustable width between armrests of 406 or 457 mm.
  • User weight capacity: pick a chair rated above the user's weight, since range also drops as the load rises.
  • Home layout: compare the chair's width and turning radius with your doors, corridors and bathroom.
  • Kerbs and steps: the Model F lists 61 mm ground clearance and 35 mm obstacle clearance, which shows how small a step these chairs can handle.

Our guide on how to choose a wheelchair covers fitting in more detail. If the user can push themselves or has a carer, a manual chair may be enough; see the folding wheelchair price guide.

What should you ask a supplier before ordering?

  1. The exact brand and model, with a link to the maker's specification page.
  2. The battery chemistry, voltage, Ah and Wh rating, with a photo of the label.
  3. The weight of the chair with and without the battery.
  4. The range figure and the user weight it was measured with.
  5. The user weight capacity, seat width and folded size.
  6. The charger's input voltage and plug, and whether it suits your home supply.
  7. Which parts can be replaced locally, such as tyres, the joystick controller and the battery.
  8. The warranty terms, in writing.

Frequently asked questions

Is a lithium-ion battery better for an electric wheelchair?

Lithium-ion batteries are used on light folding chairs such as the WHILL Model F and Pride Jazzy Passport, and they are rated in watt-hours for air travel. Compare the Wh rating, total weight and range of each model rather than the battery type alone.

How far does an electric wheelchair go on a full charge?

The makers rate the WHILL Model F at up to 20 km and the Jazzy Passport at up to about 17.7 km, depending on the battery. Real range is lower for heavier users, as Pride's own figures show.

Can I fly with a lithium battery electric wheelchair?

Usually yes, with the airline's approval in advance. Under IATA guidance a removable battery must not exceed 300 Wh, and spares go in the cabin, but check the current rule with your airline.

How heavy is a folding electric wheelchair?

The WHILL Model F weighs 26.7 kg with its battery and the Jazzy Passport about 27 kg, depending on the battery. Most people need help lifting one into a car.
