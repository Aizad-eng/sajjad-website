Portable oxygen concentrator price in Pakistan: pulse vs continuous flow

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

The portable oxygen concentrator price in Pakistan depends on whether the unit gives continuous flow as well as pulse dose, its maximum oxygen output, battery size, brand and the exchange rate. A portable oxygen concentrator, often shortened to POC, makes oxygen from room air and runs on a rechargeable battery, so the user is not tied to a cylinder or a home unit. The biggest choice is between pulse-dose units and units that also give continuous flow. This guide explains that choice, battery life and air travel rules, using manufacturer specifications and published research. It does not list prices, because they change with the model and the exchange rate, and it is not medical advice: the oxygen mode and setting are for your doctor to decide.

What sets the price of a portable oxygen concentrator in Pakistan?

The price of a portable oxygen concentrator depends mostly on whether it offers continuous flow as well as pulse dose, how much oxygen it can deliver, the battery size, the brand and the import cost on the day you order.

  • Delivery mode: pulse-only units are small; units that also give continuous flow are larger and heavier.
  • Oxygen output: the highest setting and the maximum output set what the unit can supply.
  • Battery: a standard or extended battery, and how many spares you buy.
  • Brand and model: known makers publish full specifications and user manuals.
  • Accessories: car charger, carry bag or cart, and extra cannulas.
  • Exchange rate, freight and duties: these move often, so check the current rule.

How does a portable oxygen concentrator work?

A portable oxygen concentrator separates oxygen from room air and delivers it through a nasal cannula. US rules (21 CFR 868.5440) describe a portable oxygen generator as a device that releases oxygen for respiratory therapy by chemical reaction or by physical means such as a molecular sieve, and place it in Class II. The international safety standard for this equipment is ISO 80601-2-69:2026, which covers oxygen concentrators that can be operated in transit and those that cannot.

What is the difference between pulse dose and continuous flow?

A pulse-dose unit sends a small burst of oxygen when the user breathes in, while a continuous-flow unit sends oxygen all the time at a set number of litres per minute (LPM). On the same machine, pulse mode runs far longer on a battery: CAIRE lists the Eclipse 5 at about 5.2 hours on pulse setting 2 but 2 hours on 2 LPM continuous flow.

The CAIRE SeQual Eclipse 5 shows both modes in one unit. Its continuous flow runs from 0.5 to 3.0 LPM in 0.5 LPM steps, and its pulse settings 1 to 9 deliver bursts of 16 to 192 mL. The trade-off is weight: CAIRE lists the unit at 6.8 kg plus a 1.5 kg battery. The Inogen One G5, a pulse-only unit, weighs 4.7 lb (about 2.1 kg) with a single battery and has six pulse settings.

Which mode a person needs, and at what setting, is part of their oxygen prescription. Follow your doctor's advice, and ask the doctor to confirm the chosen unit can meet it.

Is pulse setting 2 the same as 2 litres per minute?

No, a pulse setting number is not a flow rate, and the same number can deliver different amounts on different machines. A 2023 study in Respiratory Care by Casaburi and colleagues tested portable concentrators on a breathing simulator. One prescription device matched the oxygen level of 2 LPM continuous flow at pulse setting 3, another unit needed setting 4, and two low-cost over-the-counter units did not reach that level at any setting. Breathing faster also lowered the oxygen level for a given setting.

The authors wrote that oxygen needs to be prescribed individually and that some low-cost devices may not meet a user's needs. Treat any unit sold without a clear maker, model and specification sheet with caution.

How long does a portable oxygen concentrator battery last?

Battery life depends on the setting, the mode and the user's breathing rate, so compare figures measured at the same setting. Inogen states up to 6.5 hours on a single battery and up to 13 hours on a double battery for the One G5, both at flow setting 1, while Air Liquide's tests at setting 2 gave 5 and 10 hours. The Eclipse 5 figures above show how much continuous flow shortens run time.

Ask which setting a battery figure was measured at, and plan for spare batteries if the user will be away from a power socket for long. The Inogen One G5 power supply accepts 100 to 240 V at 50 to 60 Hz, which shows what to check on any unit you buy.

Can you take a portable oxygen concentrator on a plane?

Often yes, if the unit meets the airline's rules and you carry enough charged batteries. In the United States, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) accepts a POC on board when it is legally marketed under FDA rules, does not interfere with aircraft systems, does not produce compressed gas and carries no hazardous materials beyond permitted batteries. Units not on the FAA's older approved list must also carry a label in red lettering stating that they conform. The FAA's PackSafe page says users must bring enough spare batteries in carry-on baggage for the flight, protected from damage and short circuit.

Inogen states the One G5 meets the FAA acceptance criteria. Airlines flying from Pakistan set their own rules, so check the current rule with your airline before you book.

Portable or home oxygen concentrator: which do you need?

A portable unit suits a user who needs oxygen away from home, while a home (stationary) concentrator runs from mains power and is not meant to be carried. Ask your doctor which one fits the prescription. Our guides on 5 litre vs 10 litre concentrators and on whether to buy or rent an oxygen concentrator cover home units. Your doctor may also ask you to check readings with a pulse oximeter; see how to read a pulse oximeter.

What should you ask the supplier before ordering?

  1. The exact maker and model, with the specification sheet.
  2. Whether it gives pulse dose only, or continuous flow as well, and the range of settings.
  3. The maximum output and the bolus size at each pulse setting.
  4. Weight with the battery, and battery run time at the setting you will use.
  5. The battery's watt-hour rating and the price of spare batteries, if you plan to fly.
  6. The power supply's input voltage, and whether a car charger is included.
  7. Warranty terms and where filters and batteries can be replaced.
