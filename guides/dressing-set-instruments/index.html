Dressing set: which instruments it contains and what each one does

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

A basic dressing set contains three instruments: a dressing forceps, an artery (haemostatic) forceps and a pair of scissors. The Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) catalogue lists a 14.5 cm straight dressing forceps, a 14 cm straight Pean haemostatic forceps and 14 cm Deaver scissors. Wards usually add a tray, kidney dish and gallipot.

A dressing set is the smallest instrument set most clinics and wards keep, and it is often the first one a new clinic orders. Contents vary between hospitals and suppliers, so this guide uses a published reference set, the one in the Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) medical catalogue, and explains each item. It describes the equipment only. How a wound is dressed is a decision for your doctors and nurses and your facility's own procedures.

What is a dressing set?

A dressing set is a small group of instruments used to handle gauze and dressings without touching them by hand. MSF describes its dressing set as part of the minimal equipment for any health structure, containing the three basic instruments needed to make dressings in a sterile way. The instruments are reusable and are sterilised as a set between uses.

The MSF dressing set, code KSURBDRE3N-, has three instruments, one of each.

Hospitals and suppliers often build their own versions with extra forceps or a second pair of scissors. When you order, ask for a written list of contents rather than relying on the name "dressing set".

What does a dressing forceps do?

A dressing forceps is a thumb forceps, meaning it has no lock, used to pick up and move gauze, swabs and dressings. MSF classes it as a reusable forceps for fabrics and bandages. Non-locking forceps are held like a pen, and spring tension keeps the tips apart until they are squeezed. Forceps with smooth or cross-hatched tips are used for moving dressings and removing sutures, while toothed tips are meant for gripping tissue. Check which tip you are getting, because the two look alike in a photo.

What does the Pean artery forceps do in a dressing set?

The Pean forceps is a locking clamp, also called a haemostat or artery forceps. Its handles carry a ratchet, a few interlocking teeth on each handle, so the jaws stay closed without being held. Because it locks, it holds an object firmly without the user having to keep squeezing. The pattern is named after the French surgeon Jules-Émile Péan, a leading figure in the development of the modern haemostat. A 14 cm straight Pean is the size in the MSF set.

What kind of scissors are in a dressing set?

The MSF set uses 14 cm straight Deaver operating scissors with sharp/blunt tips, meaning one blade ends in a point and the other is rounded. MSF's separate dressing module adds 14.5 cm straight blunt/blunt dressing scissors, where both tips are rounded. If you want only one pair, say which tip combination you need.

What else goes with a dressing set?

A dressing set is usually used with a tray and bowls rather than on its own. The MSF dressing equipment module, intended for nursing staff, shows a typical group:

  • 2 dressing sets of 3 instruments
  • 2 extra Pean haemostatic forceps, 14 cm straight
  • 2 blunt/blunt dressing scissors, 14.5 cm straight
  • 1 stainless steel dressing tray, 30 x 20 x 3 cm
  • 1 stainless steel kidney dish, 26 x 14 cm
  • 1 stainless steel gallipot, 500 ml, 12 cm
  • 2 autoclavable plastic nail brushes

Consumables such as gauze swabs, cotton, tape and gloves are ordered separately. See our guide to gauze swabs, rolls and pads and our surgical disposables range.

How are dressing set instruments cleaned and sterilised?

Reusable dressing instruments are cleaned, wrapped and steam sterilised after every use. MSF says its three-instrument set is not supplied with a wire sterilising basket, so the instruments are wrapped in two layers of sterilisation paper before sterilisation. MSF's field guidance on sterilisation describes soaking, washing and drying the instruments, checking for corrosion or damage, oiling hinged instruments, and steam sterilising stainless steel instruments at 134 degrees C for 10 minutes. It also states that single-use items must never be reused or re-sterilised. Follow your own facility's sterilisation policy and your autoclave maker's instructions. Our sterilisation basics guide covers this in more detail.

What should you specify when ordering dressing sets?

Order by the contents of the set, not just the name. For each set, list:

  • Each instrument by name, pattern, length and straight or curved, as in the table above
  • Tip type for forceps (plain or toothed) and scissors (sharp/blunt or blunt/blunt)
  • Steel grade and finish
  • Reusable or single-use, sterile or non-sterile
  • Whether you want a tray, kidney dish, gallipot or storage box with the set
  • Number of complete sets

Frequently asked questions

What instruments are in a basic dressing set?

A basic dressing set has three instruments: a dressing forceps, an artery forceps such as a Pean, and a pair of scissors. The MSF reference set uses 14 to 14.5 cm straight instruments.

What is the difference between dressing forceps and artery forceps?

Dressing forceps are spring forceps with no lock, used to pick up and move gauze and dressings. Artery forceps, such as the Pean, have a ratchet that locks the jaws closed.

Is a kidney dish part of a dressing set?

Not always. In the MSF catalogue the kidney dish, gallipot and tray are part of a separate dressing equipment module that is used with the instrument sets.

Can dressing set instruments be reused?

Stainless steel dressing instruments are made to be cleaned and sterilised between uses. Instruments sold as single-use must never be reused or re-sterilised.

What size are dressing set instruments?

In the MSF reference set the forceps and scissors are about 14 to 14.5 cm long and straight. Other suppliers may offer different lengths, so state the size you need.
