RadioEar DD45 vs DD450 audiometric headphones: which one do you need?

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The RadioEar DD45 is a 10 ohm supra-aural headset for standard audiometry from 100 Hz to 8 kHz. The DD450 is a 40 ohm circumaural headset rated from 20 Hz to 20 kHz for high frequency testing, with more noise attenuation. They use different reference values, so an audiometer must be set up and calibrated for the one you connect.

The RadioEar DD45 and DD450 are two of the most common headsets on modern audiometers, and both come from the same Danish maker. They look different, they are wired differently and they are built for different jobs. This guide compares them using RadioEar's own datasheets and instructions, so you can tell which one your audiometer needs and what changes if you switch.

What is the main difference between the DD45 and the DD450?

The DD45 is a light supra-aural headset for standard testing up to 8 kHz, while the DD450 is a circumaural headset for standard and high frequency testing. Supra-aural cushions rest on the outer ear. Circumaural cups surround the ear. RadioEar rates the DD45 from 100 Hz to 8 kHz. It rates the DD450 from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, and states that it complies with IEC 60645-1 and ANSI S3.6 up to 16 kHz.

The two also differ electrically. The DD45 is a 10 ohm earphone and the DD450 is a 40 ohm headset, so an audiometer must be set up for the one you connect.

RadioEar's older DD45 datasheet gives a typical sensitivity of 106.7 dB SPL, and the current instructions give 108 dB plus or minus 3 dB. Both are RadioEar documents, so ask for the one that matches your unit. The DD45 datasheet also says its reference values were measured by the independent laboratories PTB and DTU, following ISO 389-9.

Why does the DD450 reach higher frequencies?

The DD450 is designed for high frequency audiometry, which tests above the standard 8 kHz limit. Its datasheet lists reference values up to 16,000 Hz and quotes Professor Emeritus Robert H. Margolis of the University of Minnesota, who says no clinically important differences are expected between results from the DD450 and the Sennheiser HDA 200. The headset alone is not enough. The DD450 datasheet also lists the maximum hearing level the headset can reach at each frequency, from 120 dB at 1000 Hz down to 60 dB at 16,000 Hz. The audiometer must also offer high frequency testing. For example, Interacoustics lists optional high frequency testing from 8 to 20 kHz on the AD629, and up to 20 kHz on the AC40.

Which headset blocks more background noise?

The DD450 blocks more background noise, because its cups close around the ear. RadioEar publishes attenuation figures for the DD450, rising from 15 dB at 125 Hz to 46 dB at 4000 Hz. It lists no attenuation figures for the DD45. If your test room is not well sound treated, the DD450's published attenuation is worth comparing.

Can you plug a DD450 into an audiometer set up for a DD45?

Not without setting up the audiometer for it. The DD45 is 10 ohm and the DD450 is 40 ohm, and the two have different reference equivalent threshold sound pressure levels (RETSPLs) at almost every frequency. At 4000 Hz, for example, RadioEar lists 12.0 dB SPL for the DD45 and 9.5 dB SPL for the DD450. If the audiometer expects one headset and you connect the other, the hearing levels it shows will be wrong.

RadioEar's DD45 instructions tell users to make sure the headset is calibrated to the audiometric equipment it will be used with before use. The same applies to the DD450. Our audiometer calibration guide explains what this involves.

Which audiometers use the DD45 and the DD450?

The DD45 is included as standard on some audiometers, and the DD450 is often offered as an option. The Interacoustics AD629 specification lists a DD45 headset as included, with the DD450, DD65v2 and TDH39 as optional headsets. The MAICO MA 1 handheld screener also ships with a DD45 headphone. If your audiometer is from another maker, check its manual for the headsets it supports and the calibration data installed. Our Interacoustics vs MAICO guide compares the audiometers themselves.

Which one is easier to use every day?

The DD45 is lighter and presses less, while the DD450 is heavier but has a padded, adjustable headband. RadioEar sells several DD45 headsets: adult versions with a foldable headband on 2 m or 5 m cords, a paediatric version on an HB3100 headband, and DD45C (contralateral) and DD45S (shielded) variants. The DD450 has replaceable ear pads, a replaceable head band and a replaceable cable, which helps in a busy clinic. RadioEar describes the DD450 cable as highly durable and easily replaceable, with gold-plated connectors.

How should you clean and maintain them?

Clean both with a soft, damp cloth and keep moisture out of the speaker. RadioEar's DD45 instructions say to avoid organic solvents and aromatic oils, and to clean the cushions with a lint-free cloth lightly dampened in cleaning solution if needed. The same document says to connect the headset only to equipment approved to IEC 60601-1 and IEC 60601-1-2, and not to use a defective device.

Wear parts can be bought separately. For the DD45, RadioEar lists red and blue or black ear cushions, 2 m and 5 m cords and a contralateral headband. For the DD450, it lists ear cushions, a replacement head band, four cable types and a replacement kit of new cushions and felt shared with the DD65v2.

Which one should a clinic in Pakistan choose?

Choose the headset your audiometer supports and that matches the tests you run.

  1. For routine air conduction testing up to 8 kHz in a quiet room or booth, the DD45 is the usual choice.
  2. For high frequency testing, choose the DD450, and confirm your audiometer has the high frequency option.
  3. For rooms with more background noise, compare the DD450's published attenuation.
  4. Check the plug type and cord length before ordering, since each headset comes in several versions.
  5. Plan who will calibrate the audiometer with the new headset, even when you replace a DD45 with another DD45.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DD450 an upgrade from the DD45?

It is a different headset for a different job. The DD450 adds high frequency testing and noise attenuation, but it only helps if your audiometer supports it and is calibrated for it.

What impedance are the DD45 and DD450?

RadioEar lists the DD45 at 10 ohm and the DD450 at 40 ohm.

Can I use a DD450 for standard audiometry?

Yes. RadioEar says the DD450 can be used for standard audiometry from 125 Hz to 8 kHz as well as high frequency testing up to 20 kHz.

How heavy is the DD450?

RadioEar lists the DD450 at 330 g complete, with a headband force of 10 N plus or minus 0.5 N.

Do I need calibration after replacing a DD45?

Yes. RadioEar's instructions say to make sure the headset is calibrated to the audiometer it will be used with before use, even when replacing like for like.
