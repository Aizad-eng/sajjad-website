How to use a nebulizer at home, step by step

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

To use a nebulizer at home, wash your hands, add only the medicine your doctor prescribed to a clean cup, connect the air tube and mouthpiece or mask, switch on and breathe in the mist with the cup upright until it sputters. Then switch off, empty the cup, and rinse and dry the parts as your manual says.

A nebulizer turns a liquid medicine into a fine mist that you breathe in through a mouthpiece or mask. Using one is simple once you know the order of steps, but small mistakes, such as tilting the cup or running the machine with a dirty filter, can mean less medicine reaches the lungs. This guide follows the instruction manuals of compressor (jet) nebulizers from Omron and Monaghan and patient guidance from National Jewish Health. It covers the equipment only. Your doctor decides which medicine you use, how much and how often.

What does a nebulizer do and what are its parts?

A compressor nebulizer pushes air through a small cup of liquid medicine and turns it into a mist that you inhale. National Jewish Health describes the system as a small plastic cup with a screw-top lid plus a source of compressed air.

On a home model such as the Omron NE-C801, the main parts are:

  • Compressor: the main unit that makes the airflow.
  • Air tube: carries air from the compressor to the cup.
  • Nebulizer kit: the medication cup, the inhalation top and a small part called the baffle that helps form the mist.
  • Mouthpiece or mask: an adult or child mask, and on some models a nosepiece.
  • Air filter: sits behind a cover on the compressor and cleans the incoming air.

Mesh and ultrasonic nebulizers work differently, so if you have one of those, follow its own manual. Our guide on mesh nebulizers explains that type.

What should you check before the first use?

Clean and disinfect the cup, mouthpiece and any mask before the very first use, because the Omron NE-C801 manual asks for this after purchase and again after a long period without use.

Before every treatment the same manual asks you to check that:

  • No part is cracked or damaged, and the nozzle and air tube are not blocked.
  • The air filter is clean. Omron says to fit a new one if it has changed colour or has been used for more than 60 days on average.
  • The kit is assembled correctly and the air tube is pushed in firmly at both ends, or air may leak.
  • The compressor is not covered by a blanket, towel or cloth, which can make it overheat.

National Jewish Health also suggests placing the compressor where it can reach the power socket and where you can reach the on and off switch while seated.

How do you set up a nebulizer step by step?

Wash your hands, add the prescribed medicine to a clean cup, connect the tubing and mouthpiece, then switch on and check for mist.

  1. Wash your hands before preparing each treatment.
  2. Open the clean nebulizer cup.
  3. Add the medicine and any other solution exactly as your doctor prescribed. Do not exceed the cup limit in your manual. The Omron NE-C801 cup takes no more than 7 mL and the Monaghan MC-300 has a maximum fill of 6 mL.
  4. Close the cup and fit the mouthpiece or mask.
  5. Connect one end of the air tube to the bottom of the cup and the other end to the compressor. Keep the cup upright while you do this so nothing spills.
  6. Switch on and check that a steady mist is coming out.

Omron warns against putting tap water or mineral water in the nebulizer for inhaling. Only use what your doctor or pharmacist has given you for the nebulizer.

How do you breathe during a nebulizer treatment?

Seal your lips around the mouthpiece, keep the cup upright and breathe in and out through the mouthpiece until the mist starts to sputter.

The Omron and Monaghan instructions both say to inhale while breathing normally and to breathe out through the mouthpiece. National Jewish Health asks for deep breaths during the treatment. If your doctor or the medicine leaflet gives a breathing pattern, follow that.

Points the manuals agree on:

  • Hold the cup upright. Omron says not to tilt it more than 45 degrees, or medicine may run into the mouth, and not to shake it while it runs.
  • Tapping the side of the cup now and then helps the liquid drop back to where it can be misted, according to National Jewish Health.
  • Carry on until the mist becomes uneven and sputters. This is the end point used by both National Jewish Health and Monaghan.

Should you use a mouthpiece or a mask?

A mouthpiece is the usual choice for adults and older children who can hold a seal, while a mask suits young children and people who cannot use a mouthpiece.

Monaghan notes that treatment can take longer with a mask. Both makers ask for close supervision when a child uses the device, because small parts can be a choking hazard.

How long can a nebulizer run in one go?

Check your own manual for a running limit, because some home compressors need rest breaks. The Omron NE-C801 manual says to limit use to 20 minutes at a time and allow a 40 minute break before using it again.

The same manual warns that the main unit can get hot during use and that continuous running may shorten the life of the device. If the compressor feels very hot, Omron lists running over 20 minutes as the likely cause. How long each treatment should last and how many treatments you need in a day is a question for your doctor.

What should you do straight after the treatment?

Switch off, disconnect the tubing, throw away any leftover medicine and rinse and dry the parts.

  1. Turn the compressor off.
  2. Pull the air tube off the bottom of the cup.
  3. Check the tube for moisture. If there is any, Omron says to leave the tube on the compressor and run it briefly to blow the moisture out.
  4. Unplug the compressor.
  5. Empty the cup. Omron says to discard leftover medicine after each use and use fresh medicine each time.
  6. Rinse and dry the parts as your manual describes.

Our separate guide on how to clean a nebulizer covers washing, disinfecting, tubing and filter changes in detail.

Why is my nebulizer not misting?

Most misting problems come from a loose connection, the wrong amount of liquid, a blocked nozzle or a tilted cup. The Omron NE-C801 troubleshooting table lists these checks:

National Jewish Health gives the same first step: check all connections, then refer to the manufacturer's instructions.

Can family members share one nebulizer?

The nebulizer kit is meant for one person. Both the Omron NE-C801 and the Monaghan MC-300 are labelled for single patient use. Omron adds that if another person does use the same device, the kit, mouthpiece and mask must be cleaned and disinfected before they use it. In practice each person should have their own kit and mouthpiece or mask.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know when a nebulizer treatment is finished?

The usual end point is when the mist becomes uneven and starts to sputter. National Jewish Health and the Monaghan MC-300 insert both use this as the sign to stop, unless your doctor tells you otherwise.

Can I put water or saline in my nebulizer?

Only put in what your doctor or pharmacist prescribed for nebulizing. The Omron NE-C801 manual warns against using tap water or mineral water in the nebulizer for inhaling.

Can I lie down while using a nebulizer?

Manuals ask you to keep the cup upright. The Omron NE-C801 should not be tilted more than 45 degrees, so sit up in a position that keeps the cup level.

Is a mask or mouthpiece better for a child?

The Monaghan MC-300 insert says a mask shall be used for patients under 5 years. The mask needs a tight seal, and a child should always be supervised during treatment.

How long can I run my nebulizer continuously?

It depends on the model. The Omron NE-C801 should run no more than 20 minutes at a time with a 40 minute rest before the next use.
