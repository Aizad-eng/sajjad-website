Surgical blade sizes and handle numbers: which blade fits which handle

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Surgical blade numbers describe shape, and each blade fits one of two handle sizes. Smaller blades such as 10, 11, 12 and 15 fit a No. 3 handle, while larger blades numbered 18 to 36, such as 20, 21, 22 and 23, fit a No. 4 handle. ISO 7740 sets these two fitting sizes so brands can interchange.

Ordering scalpel blades goes wrong in one of two ways: the blade number does not match the shape the doctor wanted, or the blades do not fit the handles you already have. This guide explains how blade numbers and handle numbers work, which blades fit which handle, and what to write on your order. Blade and handle details below come from Swann-Morton's product pages and from the ISO 7740 standard.

What do surgical blade numbers mean?

A surgical blade number identifies the blade's shape, not its length in millimetres, and each number belongs to one of two handle fitment groups.

A No. 10 blade has a curved cutting edge, a No. 11 is a pointed triangular blade, and a No. 15 is a small curved blade. Larger numbers in the 18 to 36 range are bigger blades for bigger handles. Swann-Morton groups its blades into a No. 3 range (blade shapes 6 to 16, plus the double-edged 40 and the SG3 skin graft blade) and a No. 4 range (blade shapes 18 to 36). The group tells you which handle the blade fits.

The international standard ISO 7740, Instruments for surgery, scalpels with detachable blades, fitting dimensions, sets out two sizes of fitting for detachable blades and their handles. Its stated aim is that blades from different makers and countries fit and can be swapped.

Which blades fit a No. 3 handle?

A No. 3 handle takes the smaller blades, including the common 10, 11, 12 and 15.

Swann-Morton lists these blades for its No. 3 handle: 6, 9, 10, 10A, 10R, E/11, 11, 11P, 12, 12D, 13, 14, 15, 15A, 15C, 15T, 16, 40 and SG3. Its No. 3 fitment handles include the No. 3, 3L (long), 3 Graduated, 5B, 7, 9 and B3, with B3L also listed on some blade pages. The standard No. 3 handle is stainless steel, supplied non-sterile, individually wrapped in boxes of 10, and has a measuring scale on the back.

Which blades fit a No. 4 handle?

A No. 4 handle takes the larger blades numbered 18 to 36, such as the 20, 21, 22, 23 and 24.

Swann-Morton lists these blades for its No. 4 handle: 18, 19, 20, 21, 22, 22A, 23, 24, 25, 25A, 26, 27 and 36. Its No. 4 fitment handles are the No. 4, 4L (long), 4 Graduated and 6B. The No. 4 handle is also stainless steel, non-sterile, individually wrapped and packed 10 to a box. A No. 3 blade will not fit a No. 4 handle, and a No. 4 blade will not fit a No. 3 handle, because they use the two different fitting sizes.

What are the most common blade numbers used for?

Each common blade number has a distinct cutting profile, and Swann-Morton calls the No. 15 its most popular shape for short, precise incisions.

Do blades from different brands fit the same handle?

Blades and handles made to the ISO 7740 fitting dimensions are meant to be interchangeable between makers.

That is the stated purpose of the standard, published in 1985. In practice, check that a new brand of blade sits firmly on your existing handles before you order in bulk. If a supplier cannot say which fitment their blades use, ask for a sample or a product sheet that names the handle numbers.

Should you order carbon steel or stainless steel blades?

Both are made, and the choice is usually down to the user's preference and what your clinic already uses.

Swann-Morton offers its popular blade shapes in both carbon steel and stainless steel, sterile, in individual foil packs, 100 blades to a box. For example, the No. 10 is code 0201 in carbon steel and 0301 in stainless steel. It also sells non-sterile carbon steel blades, packed as 20 foil packs of 5, which it marks for non-medical use only. For clinical work, make sure your order says sterile, and check the expiry date and the condition of the foil packs on delivery.

What about disposable scalpels, safety scalpels and blade removers?

Disposable scalpels come as a sterile blade and handle in one unit, so no separate handle is needed.

  • Disposable scalpels: sterile, single-use blade and handle units. Swann-Morton says they suit wards, emergency and trauma units and paramedics.
  • Safety scalpels: retractable designs. Swann-Morton's red-handled safety scalpels come in blade shapes 10, 10A, 10R, 11P, 12, 14, 15, 15C, 20, 21, 22, 23, 24 and 25A, and the maker links them to the EU sharps directive 2010/32/EU.
  • Blade removers: devices that take a used blade off a reusable handle without fingers touching it. Swann-Morton sells a sterile single-use remover and a multi-use unit, both for No. 3 and No. 4 handles.

Do scalpel handles come sterile?

No, the Swann-Morton No. 3 and No. 4 handles are supplied non-sterile, so they need to go through your clinic's cleaning and sterilisation process before use.

Follow the handle maker's instructions and your clinic's own procedure, and dispose of used blades through your sharps system. A blade remover avoids handling the blade with fingers when changing it. See our guides to sterilisation basics for small clinics and the minor surgery instrument set.

What should you write on a surgical blade order?

Write the blade number, material, sterile status, box quantity and the handle number you use.

  1. Blade number: for example No. 15, and the quantity per number.
  2. Material: carbon steel or stainless steel.
  3. Sterile: sterile, individually foil-packed.
  4. Box size: for example boxes of 100.
  5. Handle: No. 3, No. 4 or long versions, and how many.
  6. Extras: disposable scalpels, safety scalpels or blade removers if needed.

Frequently asked questions

What handle does a No. 15 blade fit?

A No. 15 blade fits a No. 3 handle and its variants, such as the 3L long handle. It will not fit a No. 4 handle.

What handle does a No. 22 blade fit?

A No. 22 blade fits a No. 4 handle, including the 4L long handle. All blades numbered 18 to 36 use the No. 4 fitting.

What is the difference between a No. 10 and a No. 20 blade?

Both have a curved cutting edge. Swann-Morton describes the No. 20 as a larger version of the No. 10, and the 20 fits a No. 4 handle while the 10 fits a No. 3.

Are surgical blades sterile?

Blades for clinical use are supplied sterile in individual foil packs, usually 100 to a box. Some makers also sell non-sterile blades marked for non-medical use only, so state sterile on your order.

Do disposable scalpels need a separate handle?

No. A disposable scalpel is a sterile blade and handle in one unit, so it is used as it comes and needs no No. 3 or No. 4 handle.
