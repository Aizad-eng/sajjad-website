Nitrile vs latex vs vinyl gloves: which medical gloves should you order?

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Nitrile gloves suit most clinics that want a latex-free examination glove, latex gloves suit users without latex allergy who want a natural rubber glove, and vinyl gloves are a latex-free option for short, low-risk tasks. Check the standard on the box (ASTM D6319, D3578, D5250 or EN 455), the AQL, powder-free status and whether you need examination or sterile surgical gloves.

Gloves are the item most clinics reorder most often, so small mistakes add up. This guide explains the difference between examination and surgical gloves, how nitrile, latex and vinyl compare, which standards apply, what AQL means and what to write on your order so the supplier quotes the right product.

What is the difference between examination and surgical gloves?

Examination gloves are used for medical examinations, routine procedures and handling contaminated material. They are usually bought in boxes and are often non-sterile, although the ASTM examination glove standards also cover packaged sterile versions.

Surgical gloves are sterile and used in surgical procedures. ASTM D3577 is the US specification for packaged sterile rubber surgical gloves. The US FDA lists examination gloves, surgical gloves and chemotherapy gloves as separate types, and reviews them for leak resistance, physical properties and biocompatibility before they can be sold in the US.

The quality bar is also different. Under US rules (21 CFR 800.20) the FDA treats AQL 2.5 as the minimum for patient examination gloves and AQL 1.5 for surgeons' gloves. The European standard EN 455-1 uses AQL 1.5 for examination gloves and AQL 0.65 for surgical gloves. In both systems, surgical gloves face the stricter limit.

How do nitrile, latex and vinyl gloves compare?

  • Natural rubber latex: the traditional glove material. The FDA notes that some people are allergic to the natural rubber latex used in some medical gloves, and manufacturers must list the materials on the package label.
  • Nitrile: a synthetic rubber glove with no natural rubber latex. The FDA lists nitrile among the synthetic options for people who are allergic to latex.
  • Vinyl (PVC): a synthetic, latex-free glove. The FDA also lists PVC as a synthetic alternative for latex allergy.
  • Other materials: polychloroprene examination gloves have their own ASTM standard (D6977), and the FDA mentions polyurethane as another synthetic material.

Barrier performance during use can differ by material. A widely cited 1999 study by Rego and Roley put gloves through 20 minutes of hand movements. Failure rates for latex and nitrile stayed roughly the same, while vinyl failure rates rose as high as 61 percent. Treat this as one study, not a rule for every brand, but it is a reason to think about the task before choosing the cheapest glove.

Which standards apply to each glove type?

US standards are written per material. The European standard EN 455 covers single-use medical gloves of all materials in four parts.

Glove typeUS (ASTM) standardEuropean standard
Nitrile examination glovesASTM D6319EN 455 parts 1 to 4
Natural rubber latex examination glovesASTM D3578EN 455 parts 1 to 4
Vinyl (PVC) examination glovesASTM D5250EN 455 parts 1 to 4
Polychloroprene examination glovesASTM D6977EN 455 parts 1 to 4
Sterile rubber surgical glovesASTM D3577EN 455 parts 1 to 4

ASTM D6319, D3578 and D5250 each cover gloves that fit either hand, paired gloves and gloves sold by size, in packaged sterile, packaged non-sterile and bulk non-sterile forms. D5250 also covers PVC gloves used for handling contaminated medical material.

EN 455 is split into four parts:

  • EN 455-1: freedom from holes, tested with a water leak test and AQL sampling.
  • EN 455-2: physical properties, including length, width and force at break before and after ageing.
  • EN 455-3: biological evaluation, including endotoxins on sterile gloves, powder residue on powder-free gloves and leachable proteins in latex gloves.
  • EN 455-4: how the shelf life is worked out.

Editions change over time, so ask which edition a test report refers to.

What does AQL mean on a glove box?

AQL stands for acceptable quality level. It is a statistical sampling measure used to accept or reject a production batch. The lower the AQL, the lower the share of defective gloves the batch is allowed to contain.

For holes, the test is simple. Under the FDA method, each sampled glove is filled with 1,000 ml of room-temperature water and checked straight away and again two minutes later. A leak means water appears on the outside of the glove.

Glove typeFDA (21 CFR 800.20)EN 455-1
Examination glovesAQL 2.5AQL 1.5
Surgical glovesAQL 1.5AQL 0.65

AQL does not promise that every glove in a box is free of holes. It tells you the batch passed a sampling test. When comparing quotes, a glove tested to AQL 1.5 is held to a tighter limit than one tested to AQL 2.5.

Why are powder-free gloves now the norm?

On 19 December 2016 the FDA published a final rule banning powdered surgeon's gloves, powdered patient examination gloves and absorbable powder used to lubricate surgeon's gloves. The FDA cited risks including severe airway inflammation, hypersensitivity reactions, and granulomas and adhesions that can complicate surgery.

Powder-free does not mean zero powder. Swedish public procurement criteria, for example, require powder-free medical gloves to carry no more than 2 mg of powder per glove, tested by ASTM D6124 or EN ISO 21171. EN 455-3 also covers powder residue on powder-free gloves. If you need proof, ask for the residual powder result in the test report.

What should clinics know about latex allergy when buying gloves?

The FDA advises that anyone allergic to latex, or caring for a patient who is, should choose gloves made from synthetic materials such as PVC, nitrile or polyurethane. EN 455-3 includes testing of leachable proteins in natural rubber latex gloves.

For buying, this means two practical steps. First, keep a latex-free stock even if your main glove is latex. Second, read the box label for the material and any latex statement, because the FDA requires materials to be listed on package labelling for gloves sold in the US.

How are medical gloves sized?

Examination gloves are usually ambidextrous and sold in letter sizes such as XS, S, M, L and XL. Sterile surgical gloves come in left and right pairs in numbered sizes that step in halves, for example 6, 6.5, 7, 7.5, 8 and 8.5. The exact range depends on the brand and model.

Sizes are not the same across makers. To size staff, measure around the palm at the knuckles, without the thumb, using a flexible tape, then match the result to that manufacturer's chart. Order a small mixed batch first if your team has not used the brand before.

What should you specify when ordering gloves?

A clear request gets a faster and more accurate quote. Include:

  1. Type: examination or sterile surgical.
  2. Material: nitrile, latex, vinyl or other.
  3. Sterile or non-sterile.
  4. Powder-free (and residual powder limit if you need it in writing).
  5. Standard: for example ASTM D6319 or EN 455, and the edition if your buyer requires it.
  6. AQL for freedom from holes.
  7. Sizes and quantity per size, for example 40 percent M, 30 percent S, 30 percent L.
  8. Pack size: pieces per box for examination gloves, pairs per box for surgical gloves.
  9. Length or cuff if you need longer gloves for a task.
  10. Minimum remaining shelf life on arrival, since EN 455-4 deals with how shelf life is set.

How do you check a glove supplier's paperwork?

Ask for the manufacturer's test reports against the standard printed on the box, and check that the box shows the material, size, sterile status, lot number and expiry date. In Pakistan, medical devices fall under DRAP's Medical Devices Rules, 2017. DRAP publishes provisional lists of enlisted Class A devices and registered Class B, C and D devices, along with importers. DRAP says these lists are provisional, so confirm status with the importer and with DRAP when it matters for your facility. Our guide to DRAP medical devices rules explains the system in more detail. Frequently asked questions

Are nitrile gloves better than latex gloves?

Neither is better for every task. Nitrile is latex-free, which matters if staff or patients have latex allergy, while latex is a natural rubber glove for users without that allergy. Compare the standard, AQL and thickness of the specific product.

Can vinyl gloves be used for medical examinations?

Yes, vinyl (PVC) examination gloves have their own US standard, ASTM D5250. One widely cited study found vinyl failure rates rose more than latex and nitrile during 20 minutes of hand movement, so match the glove to the task.

What AQL should medical examination gloves have?

US rules treat AQL 2.5 as the minimum for examination gloves, and EN 455-1 uses AQL 1.5. A lower number is a stricter limit.

Are powdered gloves still allowed?

The US FDA banned powdered surgeon's and patient examination gloves in a final rule published on 19 December 2016. Most buyers now order powder-free gloves and can ask for the residual powder test result.

Are examination gloves sterile?

Most examination gloves are sold non-sterile, but the ASTM examination glove standards also cover packaged sterile versions. Surgical gloves are always sterile, so state clearly which one you need.
