ECG machine price in Pakistan: channels, printing and accessories

An ECG machine, also called an electrocardiograph, records the heart's electrical activity and prints or saves it as a report. Clinics in Pakistan usually compare 3, 6 and 12 channel models, and the choice affects paper, screen, battery and running costs. This guide explains the differences using manufacturer specifications, then lists what moves the price.

What does an ECG machine record?

A standard resting ECG machine records 12 leads using 10 electrodes, four on the limbs and six on the chest. ECGbook explains that the six limb leads are I, II, III, aVR, aVL and aVF, the six chest leads are V1 to V6, and the right leg electrode acts as ground. The machine turns these signals into the familiar traces on paper or screen.

Diagnostic electrocardiographs are covered by IEC 60601-2-25, the particular standard for their basic safety and essential performance. The edition listed on the IEC webstore is 2.0, published in 2011, which merged the older IEC 60601-2-25 and IEC 60601-2-51. It excludes cardiac monitors and ambulatory ECG recorders that are not used to produce diagnostic reports.

What is the difference between 3, 6 and 12 channel ECG machines?

The channel number is how many traces the machine prints side by side at once, not how many leads it records. Many modern machines record all 12 leads at the same time and then print them in groups. Edan's specification for the SE-301, a 3-channel machine, lists simultaneous acquisition of 12 standard leads, printed on 80 mm paper.

A 12-channel machine prints all 12 traces on one wide page, usually A4 or US Letter. A 3 or 6 channel machine uses narrower paper and is smaller and lighter. For a deeper explanation, read our guide to ECG machine channels.

Do you need a printer, or is a PDF report enough?

Many current ECG machines can save and send reports digitally, so printing is now a choice rather than a must. Edan's SE-301 and SE-1200 Express specifications list PDF, XML, DICOM and FDA-SCP report formats, Wi-Fi and Ethernet, and support for some external HP laser printers. GE describes the MAC 2000 as offering paperless workflow, PDF and XML export, and connection by LAN, Wi-Fi, serial port, modem or SD card.

If your doctors read reports on a phone or computer, a smaller machine with good export may be enough. If they want a full-size printout at the bedside, a 12-channel machine with A4 printing saves time. Either way, thermal paper is a running cost, and each model takes its own size.

Which ECG accessories and consumables should you budget for?

Every ECG machine needs a patient cable, electrodes and paper, and these are the items you will reorder. Edan's SE-601 package, for example, includes adult resting tab electrodes, 10-lead universal clips and an AHA-labelled ECG cable with 4 mm banana connectors. Ask which colour and labelling scheme the cable follows so it matches what your staff know.

Electrodes come in two broad kinds: reusable suction cups and limb clamps, and single-use adhesive tab electrodes. A 2000 study of 122 hospital patients in Spain, published by the electrode maker Ambu, found that reusable cup, clamp and plate electrodes fell off in 35 percent of patients while none of the disposable ones did, with no difference in signal quality. It also found micro-organisms on reusable electrodes that cleaning did not remove. Because the study comes from the electrode maker, treat it as one view, but it shows why many clinics budget for disposable electrodes.

Other items to price are spare patient cables, thermal paper, a trolley and, for larger sites, data management software.

Which other specifications matter beyond channels?

After channels, look at how the signal is recorded, filtered and checked. Useful points to compare on the specification sheet are:

  • Simultaneous 12-lead acquisition: all leads captured at the same moment.
  • Sampling rate and frequency response: Edan lists 16,000 Hz sampling and 0.01 Hz to 300 Hz response for the SE-301 and SE-1200 Express.
  • Filters: AC filter for 50 or 60 Hz mains, baseline drift and EMG (muscle) filters.
  • Paper speeds and gain: both Edan sheets list speeds from 5 to 50 mm/s and gain settings including 10 mm/mV.
  • Defibrillation protection: both Edan sheets list CF type, defibrillation-proof patient parts.
  • Interpretation program: GE uses the Marquette 12SL program and Edan lists Glasgow interpretation on the SE-601. Automated statements support, and do not replace, a doctor's reading.
  • Standards: Edan lists IEC 60601-1, IEC 60601-1-2 and IEC 60601-2-25.

What changes the ECG machine price in Pakistan?

The price of an ECG machine in Pakistan is set mainly by channel count and features, then by brand, accessories and import costs. The main factors are:

  • Channels: 3, 6 or 12 channel printing.
  • Screen and controls: mono or colour, touch screen or keys.
  • Software: interpretation program, storage and data export formats.
  • Connectivity: Wi-Fi, Ethernet or links to hospital systems.
  • Battery: capacity and printing time away from mains.
  • Brand: makers such as GE and Edan price their ranges differently, and local service support differs.
  • Accessories: cables, electrodes, paper and a trolley.
  • Exchange rate, freight and duty: most ECG machines are imported, so the rupee rate on the order day matters.
  • Quantity: orders for several clinics can change the unit cost.

What should you ask before ordering an ECG machine?

  1. How many channels does it print, and does it record 12 leads simultaneously?
  2. What paper size does it use, and is that paper easy to reorder?
  3. Which report formats and connections are included, and which cost extra?
  4. Which interpretation program is included, if any?
  5. What comes in the box: cable type, electrodes, clips, paper?
  6. Which standards does the declaration of conformity name, including IEC 60601-2-25?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a 3 channel and a 12 channel ECG machine?

A 3 channel machine prints three traces at a time on narrow paper, while a 12 channel machine prints all 12 traces on one wide page such as A4. Many 3 channel machines still record all 12 leads at once.

How many electrodes does a 12-lead ECG use?

A standard 12-lead ECG uses 10 electrodes: four on the limbs and six on the chest. The right leg electrode acts as ground.

What paper does an ECG machine use?

ECG machines use thermal paper in a size set by the model, for example 80 mm rolls for the Edan SE-301 and 210 mm x 295 mm folded A4 packs for the Edan SE-1200 Express. Always reorder the size your model lists.

Are disposable ECG electrodes better than reusable ones?

Reusable cups and clamps can be cleaned and used again, while disposable tab electrodes are used once. A 2000 study published by the electrode maker Ambu found disposable electrodes stayed on better with similar signal quality, and found micro-organisms on reusable ones after cleaning.
