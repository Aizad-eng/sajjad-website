Surgical glove sizes: how to measure your hand and order the right size

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Surgical glove sizes run from 5.5 to 9.0 in half-size steps, and some brands also make a size 5.0. The size is based on the circumference of the palm in inches, measured over the knuckles without the thumb, so a 7 inch palm suits a size 7. Examination gloves use letter sizes XS to XL instead.

Glove size is the detail most often missing from a glove order. A box of the wrong size is wasted, and a surgical glove that is too tight or too loose makes fine work harder. This guide explains how surgical and examination glove sizes work, how to measure a hand, and how to split an order across sizes.

How are surgical glove sizes measured?

Surgical glove sizes are based on the circumference of the palm, measured in inches with the thumb left out.

A size 7 surgical glove is meant for a hand whose palm measures about 7 inches around. The general European clothing size standard EN 13402 uses a similar idea for all gloves: the largest girth over the knuckles of the open hand, fingers together and thumb excluded. Surgical gloves are sized more precisely than examination gloves because they are worn for longer and for finer work, and they are generally sterile and packed in pairs with the size printed on the pack.

How do you measure your hand for surgical gloves?

Wrap a soft tape measure around the palm of your open hand, over the knuckles, with the thumb outside the tape.

  1. Use a soft tailor's tape, not a metal tape.
  2. Open your hand flat with the fingers together. EN 13402 uses the right hand.
  3. Keep the thumb out to the side so the tape does not cover it.
  4. Wrap the tape around the widest part of the palm, across the knuckles, snug but not tight.
  5. Read the measurement in inches. If your tape is in centimetres, divide by 2.54.
  6. Match the number to the maker's chart. If it falls between two sizes, see the section below.

Measure every person who will wear the gloves and write the sizes down. One measurement per person saves guessing every time you reorder.

How are examination glove sizes different?

Examination gloves use letter sizes, generally XS, S, M and L, with some brands adding XL.

Examination gloves are sold by the box, for example 100 gloves, are often ambidextrous and come sterile or non-sterile, while surgical gloves are generally sterile and numbered. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Medical Glove Guidance Manual includes an example specification for a latex examination glove with palm widths of 80, 95 and 111 mm (plus or minus 10 mm) for small, medium and large, and a minimum length of 240 mm. That is an illustration from the manual, not a rule for every brand, but it shows why one maker's medium can be another's large. When you switch brands, order a small trial quantity first.

Which standards control glove length and width?

Glove dimensions are set by the product standards printed on the box, such as EN 455-2 in Europe and the ASTM glove specifications in the US.

  • EN 455-2 covers physical properties of single-use medical gloves, including length and width, and force at break before and after heat ageing.
  • ASTM D3577 is the US specification for packaged sterile rubber surgical gloves. Its tests include sterility, freedom from holes, physical dimensions and tensile strength.
  • Gloves for chemotherapy drugs: the FDA manual notes these are usually longer and thicker, typically at least 270 mm long and 0.10 mm thick.

Ask the supplier which standard the glove is made to and for the stated length of the size you are ordering. For material standards such as ASTM D6319 for nitrile and EN 455 in full, see our guide to nitrile vs latex vs vinyl gloves.

What if you are between two glove sizes?

Try both sizes before ordering in bulk, because palm thickness and finger length also affect fit.

People with a thicker palm may need one size larger than their measurement, and people with a thin palm may need one size smaller. First-time users of surgical gloves often need some trial before settling on a size and brand. Ask your supplier for a few pairs of each size you are unsure about, wear them for a real task, and record the size that gives full finger reach without loose material at the tips or tightness across the palm.

Why does surgical glove size matter?

The wrong glove size reduces dexterity and comfort, and comfort affects whether staff keep their gloves on.

Hand size itself also matters for how instruments feel. A 2004 survey of 726 laparoscopic surgeons by Berguer and Hreljac, published in Surgical Endoscopy, grouped respondents by glove size and found that those in the small glove group reported difficulty with laparoscopic instruments for a greater share of the time than the medium and large groups. The study is about hand size, not glove fit, but it shows why a range of sizes needs to be stocked rather than one size for everyone.

How should a clinic split a glove order across sizes?

Base the split on the measured sizes of your staff and on what you actually used, not on a fixed percentage.

  1. List each person who wears surgical gloves and their measured size.
  2. Count the pairs of each size used over the last three months, if you have records.
  3. Order by size in proportion to that use, plus a margin for delivery time.
  4. Keep a small stock of the sizes used by visiting doctors or new staff.
  5. Check expiry dates when stock arrives and use older stock first.

Examination gloves follow the same logic with letter sizes. For the rest of your order, see the surgical disposables checklist for clinics.

What should you write on a surgical glove order?

Write the glove type, material, size split, sterile status, powder status, pack size and standard.

  1. Type: sterile surgical gloves or examination gloves.
  2. Material: natural rubber latex or a synthetic material such as nitrile. The FDA notes that some people are allergic to natural rubber latex, so state if any user has latex allergy.
  3. Sizes and quantities: for example 20 boxes of 7.0, 15 of 6.5 and 10 of 7.5.
  4. Powder-free: the FDA banned powdered surgeon's and patient examination gloves in a 2016 final rule.
  5. Pack size: pairs per box for surgical gloves, pieces per box for examination gloves.
  6. Standard: for example ASTM D3577 or EN 455.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know my surgical glove size?

Measure around the palm of your open hand, over the knuckles, without the thumb. The number in inches is roughly your surgical glove size, so a 7 inch palm usually means a size 7, but check the maker's chart.

What is the most common surgical glove size range?

Surgical gloves typically come in sizes 5.5 to 9.0 in half-size steps. Some brands also make a size 5.0.

Is a size 7 surgical glove the same as a medium examination glove?

Not exactly. Surgical gloves use number sizes based on palm circumference, while examination gloves use letter sizes that differ between brands, so check each maker's chart.

Do all brands fit the same in the same size?

No. Fit can vary between brands, materials and glove shapes even at the same size, so order a few pairs to try before buying in bulk.

Are surgical gloves sold singly or in pairs?

Sterile surgical gloves are packed as a pair with the size printed on the pack, and boxes list pairs per box. Examination gloves are usually sold as loose gloves by the box.
