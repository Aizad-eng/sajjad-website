A suction machine draws secretions and fluids out of the airway or a surgical site through tubing into a collection jar. Buyers in Pakistan usually choose between a small battery portable unit, a larger mains-powered ward or theatre pump, and a low-flow unit for newborn care. This guide explains how they differ, which specifications to compare and what moves the price.

What is a suction machine and where is it used?

A suction machine is a pump that creates negative pressure to pull fluid through tubing into a container. Medela, for example, lists its Dominant Flex pump for general surgery, endoscopy, airway suction, neurosurgery and wound drainage in hospitals, clinics and doctors' practices, and also as a backup to wall suction. WHO's technical specifications describe smaller suction machines used to clear mucus from the airways of newborns and infants.

The main international standard is ISO 10079-1:2022, Medical suction equipment, Part 1: Electrically powered suction equipment. According to the BSI summary, it sets safety and performance requirements for electrically powered medical and surgical suction equipment used in hospitals, in home care, and in field and transport use.

What is the difference between a portable and a ward suction machine?

A portable suction machine runs on a rechargeable battery and is light enough to carry, while a ward or theatre unit runs on mains power and is built for higher flow and longer sessions. The Laerdal Compact Suction Unit 4 (LCSU 4) is a typical portable example. Laerdal's product listing describes a 12 V nickel metal hydride battery with up to 45 minutes of continuous use on free flow suction, an AC/DC adapter charger, and a 300 ml canister with an 800 ml option.

The Medela Dominant Flex is a typical ward and theatre example. Medela's technical factsheet lists a maximum vacuum of minus 95 kPa (713 mmHg), three user-selectable free flow rates of at least 40, 50 and 60 litres per minute, 120 W power consumption and a weight of 9.3 kg for the rack version. It also comes as a portable version and a trolley version, which weighs 16 kg.

As a rule of thumb, portable units suit ambulances, home care and bedside backup, while ward units suit theatres, emergency rooms and wards without reliable wall suction. A mains-only unit stops in a power cut, so ask about battery or UPS options if load shedding affects your site.

How are vacuum and flow different, and why do both matter?

Vacuum is how strongly the machine pulls, in mmHg or kPa, and flow is how much air it moves each minute, in litres per minute. They are separate numbers, so a unit can score well on one and not the other. Medela's factsheet shows how flow affects use: its 60 l/min setting gives faster vacuum build-up, while the 40 l/min setting lowers noise for sensitive procedures.

Higher is not always better. WHO notes that high vacuum pressures can harm a newborn's airway tissues, and its newborn suction machine specification sets a maximum of 100 mmHg. The right setting for any patient is a clinical decision, so follow your doctor's advice and your facility's procedures. When buying, check that the unit has a vacuum regulator and a clear gauge so staff can set and read the level.

Why do collection jars, filters and overflow protection matter?

Overflow protection stops fluid from reaching the pump, which protects the motor and limits contamination. WHO's specification asks for a pump that stops when the container is full, or an overfill device that stops the machine once no more than 5 ml of liquid has passed it, plus a means to keep the pump from being contaminated. Medela offers fluid collection systems with integrated overflow protection and an integrated bacterial filter in the disposable liner.

Jar size decides how often staff must empty or change it. Disposable liners are an ongoing cost, while reusable jars need washing and disinfection between uses. WHO also specifies patient tubing at least 0.5 m long and of a non-collapsible type, and it states that suction catheters are single use and must be discarded after use.

Which standards should a suction machine meet?

Look for ISO 10079-1 for electrically powered suction equipment and IEC 60601-1 for general electrical safety. BSI lists ISO 10079-1:2022 as current, replacing the 2015 edition and its 2019 amendment, with the general requirements now referenced to ISO 10079-4:2021. Medela's factsheet states that the Dominant Flex fulfils IEC 60601-1:2005 Edition 3.2 and is Class IIa under EU Regulation 2017/745.

Older documents may cite earlier editions. WHO's newborn specification, for example, names ISO 10079-1:1999 as a minimum. Ask the supplier which editions the declaration of conformity names. For local rules on importing devices, see our guide to the DRAP Medical Devices Rules.

What changes the suction machine price in Pakistan?

The price of a suction machine in Pakistan is set mainly by its type and performance, then by brand, accessories and import costs. The main factors are:

  • Type: battery portable, mains ward unit or trolley unit.
  • Performance: maximum vacuum, free flow and whether flow is selectable.
  • Collection system: one or two jars, jar size and use of disposable liners.
  • Power: battery, mains or both, and battery run time.
  • Brand and origin: for example, Medela states its Dominant Flex is made in Switzerland. Origin and brand support affect the cost.
  • Accessories: tubing, filters, liners, catheters, spare jars and a trolley.
  • Exchange rate and import costs: most units are imported, so the rupee rate, freight and duty on the order day matter. See our guide to customs duty on medical equipment.
  • Quantity: ordering several units for a ward or ambulance fleet can change the unit cost.

What should you ask before ordering a suction machine?

  1. Is it battery portable or mains only, and how long does the battery run?
  2. What are the maximum vacuum (mmHg or kPa) and free flow (l/min)?
  3. How many jars come with it, what size, and do disposable liners fit?
  4. What overflow protection and filter does it use, and what do spare filters cost?
  5. Which standards does the declaration of conformity name, including ISO 10079-1 and IEC 60601-1?
  6. Does the mains voltage and plug suit your site?
  7. What warranty applies, and are spare parts available?

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a portable and a ward suction machine?

A portable suction machine runs on a rechargeable battery and is light enough to carry, which suits transport and home care. A ward or theatre unit runs on mains power and usually offers higher flow and larger jars for longer use.

Can a suction machine be used at home?

Yes. ISO 10079-1 covers electrically powered suction equipment used in home care as well as in hospitals and in transport. How and when it is used for a patient should follow your doctor's advice.

How long does a portable suction machine battery last?

It depends on the model. Laerdal lists up to 45 minutes of continuous free flow suction for the LCSU 4, so ask for the stated run time of the exact model you are quoted.

What is overflow protection on a suction machine?

Overflow protection stops fluid from passing the collection jar into the pump, for example through an automatic cut-off, an overflow valve or a filter. It protects the pump and helps prevent contamination.
