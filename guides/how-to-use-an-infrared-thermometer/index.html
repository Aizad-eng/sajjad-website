How to use an infrared thermometer correctly: forehead, no-touch and ear models

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

To use an infrared thermometer correctly, keep the thermometer and the person in the same room for 10 to 30 minutes, check the forehead is clean, dry and uncovered, hold the sensor straight and still as the manual describes, and repeat the reading. Ear models need a new lens filter every time. Follow the manual for your exact model.

Infrared thermometers are quick and easy, which is why clinics, schools and families use them. They are also easy to use wrongly. A cold room, a sweaty forehead or a hand that moves can change the number on the screen. This guide explains the steps that manufacturers and the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) describe, so you get a reading you can trust. It is about using the device, not about what a reading means for anyone's health. For that, follow your doctor's advice.

What is an infrared thermometer and how does it measure temperature?

An infrared thermometer measures the heat (infrared energy) given off by the skin and turns it into a temperature on the screen. There are three common types:

  • Touch forehead thermometers rest flat on the centre of the forehead. The Braun BFH175 is one example. Its manual says it reads the infrared energy from the skin and nearby tissue and shows an "oral equivalent" figure, meaning it adjusts the forehead value to match a mouth reading.
  • Non-contact infrared thermometers are held a short distance from the forehead. The FDA says the correct distance depends on the device, so check the manual.
  • Ear (tympanic) thermometers have a probe that goes into the ear canal, such as the Braun ThermoScan 7. They use a disposable lens filter over the probe.

How should you prepare the room and the thermometer?

Use the thermometer in a room with a steady temperature, and let the device settle in that room before you measure. The FDA advises using non-contact thermometers in a draft-free space, out of direct sunlight and away from radiant heat. Its guidance gives an environment of 16 to 40 °C with relative humidity below 85 percent, and says to keep the thermometer in the room for 10 to 30 minutes before use.

Manufacturer limits can be narrower. The Braun BFH175 manual lists an operating range of 15 to 40 °C and shows an "Err" message if the room is outside that range. It also says that if the thermometer was stored somewhere cooler or warmer, it needs 10 minutes to adjust before use. The ThermoScan 7 manual lists an operating range of 10 to 40 °C.

How should you prepare the person before measuring?

The forehead should be clean, dry and uncovered, and the person should have been resting indoors for a while. The FDA says the forehead area should not be blocked, and that headbands, bandanas, heavy clothing and facial cleansing products can affect the reading.

The Braun BFH175 manual adds more detail:

  • The person should be inside for 30 minutes before a measurement.
  • No eating, drinking or physical activity before or during the reading.
  • Remove hats and wait 10 minutes.
  • Wipe away sweat with a dry cloth. If you clean the forehead or move hair away, wait 10 minutes.
  • Do not use cooling or warming cloths on the forehead for at least 30 minutes before measuring.
  • Do not measure over scars, open sores or scrapes.
  • Do not measure a baby while or straight after feeding.

How do you take a forehead reading step by step?

Hold the thermometer in the right spot, keep it still and wait for the signal before you move it. Using the Braun BFH175 touch model as the example:

  1. Remove the protective cap from the sensor.
  2. Switch the thermometer on and wait until it shows it is ready (three dashes on this model).
  3. Place it flat on the centre of the forehead, just above and between the eyebrows.
  4. Press the measure button and keep both the thermometer and the head still.
  5. Do not lift it until it beeps. This model gives one long beep or a series of short beeps.
  6. Read the number, then put the cap back on.

For non-contact models, the FDA says to hold the sensor at a right angle to the forehead, keep the person still, use the distance stated in the manual and never touch the sensor window. Holding it at a slant, or using it in direct sun, are shown by the FDA as common mistakes.

How do you use an infrared ear thermometer?

Fit a new, clean lens filter, make sure the ear is clear, place the probe steadily and use the same ear every time. The ThermoScan 7 manual says the thermometer will not work without a lens filter, and that a new, clean filter is needed to avoid wrong readings. It also says the ear must be free of blockages and heavy earwax, and that the probe must sit correctly and stay steady. This model has an age setting (Braun calls it Age Precision) that stays on the last age used unless you change it, so check it before each use if more than one person shares the device.

Keep a stock of the correct lens filters for your model. Filters from another brand may not fit.

Why do infrared readings vary, and how many readings should you take?

Readings vary mainly because of movement, position, the room and the skin surface, so it helps to take more than one. The Braun BFH175 manual recommends taking three readings in the same spot and using the highest one for newborns in their first 100 days, for young children with weak immunity, and while you are still learning to use the thermometer. It also suggests learning each person's normal reading by measuring twice a day (early morning and late afternoon) when they are well, and it advises checking with a standard digital thermometer for confirmation, especially for infants.

What do the accuracy figures and standards on the box mean?

They tell you how close the thermometer should be to the true value in lab tests, and which test rules it was built to. The Braun BFH175 lists a measuring range of 34 to 43 °C, a display step of 0.1 °C and lab accuracy of 0.2 °C between 35 and 42 °C (0.3 °C outside that band). Its manual says it meets ASTM E1965, and explains that ASTM allows infrared thermometers plus or minus 0.3 °C in the 36 to 39 °C range, compared with plus or minus 0.1 °C for electronic contact thermometers.

Other standards you may see are ISO 80601-2-56 (safety and performance of clinical thermometers), IEC 60601-1 (electrical safety) and IEC 60601-1-11 (medical devices used at home). The FDA lists ASTM E1965 and ISO 80601-2-56 as reference standards for non-contact thermometers. Our guide to thermometer accuracy standards explains these in more depth.

Which thermometer type do health bodies suggest for young children?

For babies and young children, UK guidance favours electronic thermometers under the arm, with infrared ear thermometers as an option after 4 weeks of age. The National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guideline NG143 says to use an electronic thermometer in the armpit for infants under 4 weeks, and for children aged 4 weeks to 5 years lists an electronic thermometer in the armpit, a chemical dot thermometer in the armpit or an infrared ear thermometer. It also says forehead chemical (strip) thermometers are unreliable and should not be used by healthcare professionals. The NHS page on fever in children advises a digital thermometer placed at the top of the armpit.

So a forehead infrared thermometer is a quick screening tool, but many families also keep a digital stick thermometer. See infrared vs digital thermometer for a side-by-side comparison, and follow your doctor's advice on which reading to rely on.

How do you clean and store an infrared thermometer?

Wipe it, never soak it, and keep the sensor covered when it is not in use. The FDA notes that most non-contact thermometers should never be put in water or other liquids, and that you should follow the cleaning section of the manual. The Braun BFH175 manual says to wipe the casing and sensor with a 70% isopropyl alcohol swab for 90 seconds, then let it air-dry for 10 minutes before the next reading. It warns against abrasive cleaners, thinners and benzene, and says to store the device with its cap on, between minus 25 and 55 °C.

Replace batteries when the low battery symbol appears. The BFH175 uses two AAA batteries, which Braun rates at about 500 measurements.

Frequently asked questions

How long should an infrared thermometer sit in the room before use?

The FDA suggests 10 to 30 minutes in the room where you will measure. The Braun BFH175 manual says 10 minutes if it was stored somewhere cooler or warmer, and that the person should be indoors for 30 minutes.

Why does my forehead thermometer give different readings each time?

Small changes in position, movement, sweat, hair or room temperature change the reading. Hold it straight and still in the spot the manual shows, and take two or three readings.

Do ear thermometers need a new lens filter every time?

For the Braun ThermoScan 7, yes. Its manual says the thermometer will not work without a filter and that a new, clean filter is needed for accurate readings.

Can I clean an infrared thermometer with water?

No. The FDA says most non-contact thermometers should never be put in water or other liquids. Wipe the casing and sensor as the manual describes, for example with a 70% isopropyl alcohol swab.

Is an infrared thermometer good for babies?

UK NICE guidance recommends an electronic thermometer under the arm for infants under 4 weeks, and lists infrared ear thermometers as one option from 4 weeks to 5 years. Follow your doctor's advice for your child.
