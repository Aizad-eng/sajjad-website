Accu-Chek Guide vs Instant vs Active glucometers: how the three meters differ

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Accu-Chek Guide and Accu-Chek Instant are Roche meters with Bluetooth, about 4 second results and at least 720 stored results. The Guide adds a strip ejector and port light, the Instant a colour target range bar. The long-running Accu-Chek Active needs 1 to 2 µL, takes about 5 seconds and allows out-of-meter dosing. Each uses its own strips.

Accu-Chek glucometers are made by Roche, and three names come up most often: Accu-Chek Guide, Accu-Chek Instant and Accu-Chek Active. They all measure blood glucose from a finger prick, but they differ in speed, memory, phone connection and how blood goes on the strip. This guide compares them using Roche's own product pages and manual. It covers the equipment only. Targets and treatment are for your doctor to set.

What is the main difference between Accu-Chek Guide, Instant and Active?

The Guide and Instant have Bluetooth and give results in about 4 seconds, while the Active is a long-running design, with a UK manual dating from 2006, that reads a colour change on the strip and lets you put blood on the strip outside the meter. In short:

  • Accu-Chek Guide: strip ejector, strip port light, backlit screen, Bluetooth to the mySugr app, 720 records.
  • Accu-Chek Instant: colour-coded target range indicator, Bluetooth to mySugr, at least 720 results, 0.6 µL sample.
  • Accu-Chek Active: 1 to 2 µL sample, about 5 seconds, out-of-meter dosing, infrared data port on older versions.

Which test strips does each Accu-Chek meter use?

Each meter is paired with its own strip, so buy the strip with the same name as your meter. Roche's Instant page names the Accu-Chek Instant test strip, and the Active manual warns that any other strips will give incorrect results. Check the strip name printed on your meter box before ordering refills.

This matters more than the meter price, because strips are what you buy every month. Before choosing a meter, make sure its strips are easy for you to get. Our guide to glucometer strips in Pakistan covers compatibility and expiry.

How accurate are Accu-Chek Guide, Instant and Active?

Roche's US page for the Guide says 95% of results fall within plus or minus 10 mg/dL of the lab reference below 100 mg/dL, and within 10% at 100 mg/dL and above, based on Roche's data on file. The UK Instant page does not quote an accuracy figure and points to the package insert. Our guide to glucometer accuracy and ISO 15197 explains the international standard meters are tested against.

Other conditions also matter. The Instant page lists a haematocrit range of 10% to 65%, an operating range of 4 to 45 degrees Celsius and 10% to 90% humidity, and use up to 3,094 metres altitude. Testing outside those conditions is not covered by the specification.

What is out-of-meter dosing on the Accu-Chek Active?

Out-of-meter dosing means you can take the strip out after starting a test, put blood on it, and push it back in. The Active manual says you then have 20 seconds to apply blood and reinsert the strip, and the result takes about 10 seconds in this mode instead of about 5.

The manual presents this as useful for testing by health professionals, for example from an earlobe, and says to use a separate lancet for each patient. The Active also reads a colour change on the strip with an optical system, and the manual describes a rough colour comparison that must be done within 30 to 60 seconds, only as a plausibility check.

What does the Accu-Chek Instant target range indicator show?

The Instant has a colour-coded bar on the screen that shows whether a reading is high, low or within your target range. Roche's UK page describes it as a colour-coded target range indicator, and the meter connects to the mySugr app over Bluetooth.

The target range itself should be agreed with your doctor. The indicator is a reading aid, not a diagnosis.

Do the Accu-Chek meters connect to a phone?

The Guide and Instant connect to the mySugr app over Bluetooth, according to Roche. The Guide also has a micro USB port. The older Active described in the 2006 manual uses an infrared port to send data to a computer with suitable software.

If you want readings on your phone, check that the mySugr app supports your phone and is available in your app store before buying. If you only need a number on the screen, any of the three works.

Is there a recall or notice on the Accu-Chek Guide?

Roche's US Guide page carries a notice about nine Guide meters that were set to show results in mmol/L instead of mg/dL. The page lists the affected serial numbers and says to stop using those meters.

This is a reminder for any meter: check which unit the screen shows, mg/dL or mmol/L, and make sure it matches the unit your doctor uses. Write it down in your logbook.

Which Accu-Chek meter should you choose?

Choose on the features you will use every day and on strip supply, not on brand alone:

  1. Want readings on your phone: Guide or Instant.
  2. Test in low light: the Guide has a strip port light and backlit screen.
  3. Want a quick high, low or in-range cue: the Instant's target range indicator.
  4. Clinic or carer use with out-of-meter dosing: the Active.
  5. Every case: confirm the strip name, strip expiry date, the unit on the screen, and that an English manual is in the box.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use Accu-Chek Instant strips in an Accu-Chek Guide meter?

No. Each Accu-Chek meter is paired with its own named strip. Use only the strip named for your meter, as Roche warns other strips can give incorrect results.

Which Accu-Chek meter is fastest?

Roche lists the Guide at under 4 seconds and the Instant at about 4 seconds. The Active takes about 5 seconds, or about 10 seconds with out-of-meter dosing.

Does the Accu-Chek Active connect to a phone?

The Active described in Roche's 2006 manual has an infrared port for a computer, not Bluetooth. The Guide and Instant connect to the mySugr app over Bluetooth.

How much blood does Accu-Chek Instant need?

Roche's UK page lists a minimum sample of 0.6 µL for the Instant. The Active needs 1 to 2 µL, according to its manual.

How many readings does the Accu-Chek Guide store?

Roche's US page lists 720 diary records and 32 control results for the Guide.
