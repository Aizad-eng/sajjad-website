Every blood sugar test on a glucometer uses a new strip, so strips are the item you buy again and again. That makes it worth knowing which strips fit your meter, how long they last and how to store them. This guide covers compatibility, expiry, storage and safe buying for glucometer strips in Pakistan. It does not tell you how often to test; follow your doctor's advice on that.

Which strips work with my glucometer?

Only the strips made for your exact meter model will work with it. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) tells users to buy test strips designed for their own meter. Brands make several meter lines, and strips are often not shared between them. Roche, for example, states that Accu-Chek Instant strips are for the Accu-Chek Instant meter only, so they are not interchangeable with Accu-Chek Guide or Active strips.

Before ordering, read the full model name on the meter and on your last strip box. "Accu-Chek" or "On Call" alone is not enough. Our comparison of Accu-Chek and On Call Plus glucometers show how the families differ.

What do the specifications on a strip leaflet mean?

The leaflet lists the blood sample size, the test time, the measuring range and the conditions the strip works in. Roche, for example, gives these figures for Accu-Chek Instant strips: a 0.6 microlitre blood sample, a result in under 4 seconds, a measuring range of 0.6 to 33.3 mmol/L and a haematocrit range of 10 to 65 percent. It also notes that very high blood fats (triglycerides above 1,800 mg/dL) may raise the result.

These numbers help when comparing systems, but they only apply to the matching meter. Read them before you switch brands, and ask your doctor if any limitation applies to you.

How long do glucometer strips last after opening?

It depends on the brand: some strips stay usable until the printed expiry date even after the vial is opened, while others must be thrown away a set time after first opening. Roche's performance sheet for Accu-Chek Instant strips gives a stability of 21 months from production and says they remain stable after opening until the expiry date printed on the vial, as long as the container is closed tightly after each strip is removed.

Other brands may print a shorter limit after first opening. Always read the leaflet in the box, and write the opening date on the vial if the leaflet gives a discard period. The FDA warns that expired strips can give incorrect results.

How should you store test strips in hot weather?

Store strips within the temperature range on the box, in their original container with the cap closed, away from heat and moisture. The table below shows the published ranges for two strip types.

Do not keep strips in a car, on a window sill or next to a stove. If your home gets hotter than the printed limit, keep the vial in the coolest part of the home and follow the storage advice in the leaflet.

Are cheap or loose glucometer strips safe to buy?

Strips that are pre-owned, repacked or sold without a clear source are not safe to rely on. In April 2019 the FDA warned that pre-owned strips or strips not authorised for sale can give wrong results and can carry a risk of infection from a previous user's blood. It also noted that sellers may change or cover the expiry date.

  • Check the seal: do not accept a vial or box that looks opened or altered.
  • Check the date: make sure the printed expiry date is clear and not covered.
  • Compare with past boxes: the FDA lists strips that look different from others of the same brand as a warning sign.
  • Buy new, sealed stock: the FDA advises buying new, unopened vials from a trusted source.

What accuracy standard should glucometer strips meet?

Strips are tested together with their meter under ISO 15197:2013, the international standard for blood glucose monitoring systems used for self-testing. It requires at least 95 percent of results to fall within plus or minus 15 mg/dL of the laboratory value below 100 mg/dL, or within plus or minus 15 percent at 100 mg/dL and above. At least 99 percent must also fall in zones A and B of the consensus error grid. The European Union adopted the same rules as EN ISO 15197:2015.

Accuracy testing under the standard uses three different strip lots, because strips can vary from batch to batch. Ask whether the meter and strip system you are buying states compliance with ISO 15197:2013. Our guide to glucometer accuracy and ISO 15197 explains the numbers further.

Do you need control solution as well as strips?

Control solution is worth having because it lets you check that the meter and strips are working together. The FDA advises regular testing with a control solution. Your meter's manual says how often and which solution to use, and the solution must match the strip brand. Questions about what a reading means are for your doctor.

How many strips should you order at once?

Order only as many strips as you can use before the printed expiry date, or before the open-vial limit if the brand has one. Buying in bulk lowers the unit price only if the stock is used in time. For a clinic or pharmacy, it helps to:

  1. Work out how many strips you use per month.
  2. Ask for the expiry date of the batch before you confirm the order.
  3. Store boxes so the oldest batch is used first.
  4. Keep the meter model list for every patient group you supply, so strips are never mixed up.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use any brand of strips with my glucometer?

No. Strips are made for specific meter models, and the FDA advises using strips designed for your meter. Roche, for example, says Accu-Chek Instant strips work only with the Accu-Chek Instant meter.

Can I use glucometer strips after the expiry date?

No. The FDA warns that expired strips can give incorrect results. Some brands also set a shorter limit after the vial is first opened, so check the leaflet.

How should I store glucometer strips?

Keep them in the original container with the cap closed, within the printed temperature range, away from heat and moisture. Roche lists 4 to 30 °C for Accu-Chek Instant strips and says not to freeze them.

Are second-hand or loose strips safe?

The FDA warned in 2019 that pre-owned or unauthorised strips can give wrong results, may carry infection risk and may have altered expiry dates. Buy new, sealed boxes from a known source.

What accuracy standard applies to glucometer strips?

ISO 15197:2013 requires at least 95 percent of results within plus or minus 15 mg/dL below 100 mg/dL, or plus or minus 15 percent at 100 mg/dL and above. It is tested on the meter and strips together.
