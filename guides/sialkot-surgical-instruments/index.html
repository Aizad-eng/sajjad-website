Sialkot surgical instruments: the industry and how to buy from it

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Sialkot in Punjab makes almost all of Pakistan's surgical instruments. The Trade Development Authority of Pakistan (TDAP) says over 2,500 companies there produce around 10,000 instrument types and sell about 95% abroad. The Surgical Instruments Manufacturers Association of Pakistan (SIMAP) reported exports of about US$445 million in 2025. Buyers should specify pattern, size and steel grade.

Almost all surgical instruments made in Pakistan come from one city. This guide sets out what official and industry sources say about the Sialkot surgical cluster, with the year each figure refers to, and then explains how a clinic or hospital in Pakistan can order instruments from it. Figures change, so treat every number as correct at the time of writing (November 2026).

Why is Sialkot known for surgical instruments?

Sialkot is the centre of Pakistan's surgical instrument industry, with a history TDAP puts at more than 100 years. TDAP describes the city as the largest producer of surgical, dental and veterinary instruments in the world, and says almost all of Pakistan's surgical production comes from there. The industry is export led. TDAP estimates that Pakistan's total production is roughly equal to its exports, and that about 95% of Sialkot's products are sold internationally.

How much does Pakistan export in surgical instruments?

Pakistan's surgical instrument exports were roughly US$420 million to US$490 million a year between 2021 and 2025. Trade Map data reproduced by TDAP shows exports of about US$366 million in 2017 and about US$421 million in 2021. SIMAP figures reported by Arab News in January 2026 put exports at about US$420 million in 2021, nearly US$492 million in 2024 and roughly US$445 million in 2025.

The same TDAP data lists the largest buyers in 2021 as the United States (about US$113 million), Germany (about US$62 million), the United Kingdom (about US$37 million), China (about US$20 million) and the UAE (about US$18 million). Almost all of the value, 98.92%, falls under HS code 901890, the customs heading for general medical, surgical and veterinary instruments. Needles, catheters and cannulae (HS 901839) made up 0.73% and dental instruments (HS 901849) 0.16%, which shows how strongly the cluster is focused on reusable hand instruments.

Why have Sialkot's exports fallen recently?

SIMAP blames tax changes and rising costs for the drop from 2024 to 2025. Its chairman, Dr Zeeshan Tariq, told Arab News that the 2024 move from the Final Tax Regime to the National Tax Regime brought documentation that small family workshops struggle with. He also pointed to higher electricity prices, the cost of international compliance for small firms and an ageing workforce, saying the average worker's age has risen from 25 to 27 years to about 45 over two decades.

What is SIMAP and what does it do?

The Surgical Instruments Manufacturers Association of Pakistan (SIMAP) is the trade body that represents Sialkot's instrument makers. TDAP describes it as responsible for promoting the craft and helping solve the industry's problems. SIMAP is based on Head Marala Road in Sialkot. Its website lists services for members such as visa recommendation letters, document attestation, trade delegations, exhibitions and help with trade disputes. It also has a members directory that can be searched by member ID, and a page for blacklisted companies. A buyer can use the directory to check that a maker who claims membership is listed.

What is SIMTEL?

SIMTEL is the Sialkot Material Testing Laboratory, set up in 2001 and run jointly by TDAP and SIMAP. Its website says it was established under the guidance of the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA). It tests the elemental content of stainless steels, advises makers on the right grade, such as AISI 304 or 316L, and investigates failed samples using spectrometry, chemical tests and microstructure analysis. For a buyer, this means a Sialkot maker can have its steel tested locally, so asking for a material test report is a reasonable request. What rules apply to surgical instrument makers in Pakistan?

TDAP's brochure lists the basic steps. A maker registers its company with the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP), then with the Federal Board of Revenue (FBR). For most products sold inside Pakistan, it also needs to register with the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP). For export, buyers in the EU and USA expect CE marking or FDA registration, and each importing country has its own rules. TDAP adds that testing and product approvals are mostly set by the buyer or the importing country.

DRAP publishes lists of enlisted Class A and registered Class B, C and D devices, and of licensed manufacturers and importers, on its website. DRAP calls some of these lists provisional, so check the current rule. Our DRAP medical devices rules guide covers the system in more detail.

How do you buy surgical instruments from Sialkot?

Buy by exact description, not by photo or name alone, because the same instrument name can come in many lengths, patterns and finishes. Most Sialkot firms are built around export orders, and TDAP notes that local supply chains and distribution are limited. A buyer inside Pakistan can approach a maker directly, go through a distributor, or use a sourcing service. Whichever route you choose:

  1. Write a specification for each item (see the list below).
  2. Check the maker is listed in the SIMAP members directory if it claims membership.
  3. Ask for a sample or photos of the actual item before a large order.
  4. Ask for the steel grade and, for a larger order, a material test report.
  5. Confirm whether instruments come sterile or non-sterile and how they are packed. TDAP says makers sterilise and pack to the buyer's request.
  6. Agree the labelling. TDAP notes Pakistani makers ship with their own labels or the buyer's.

TDAP also notes that packing depends on the product. Some instruments go in sterilised airtight plastic covers, some in hard boxes and more delicate ones in specialised boxes. Some makers offer titanium coating on request. State any of these in writing, because they change the price and the delivery time.

What should you specify when ordering instruments?

A good specification lets two makers quote for the same item. For each instrument, list:

  • Instrument name and pattern, for example Pean artery forceps or Mayo scissors
  • Length in cm, and straight or curved
  • Jaw or tip type, such as serrated, toothed, sharp or blunt
  • Steel grade and surface finish
  • Reusable or single-use, sterile or non-sterile
  • Quantity per pattern, and whether you want sets or loose pieces

Frequently asked questions

Why is Sialkot famous for surgical instruments?

Sialkot has made surgical instruments for more than 100 years, according to TDAP, and almost all of Pakistan's surgical production comes from the city. TDAP describes it as the world's largest producer of surgical, dental and veterinary instruments.

How many surgical instrument companies are in Sialkot?

How much are Pakistan's surgical instrument exports worth?

SIMAP figures reported in January 2026 put exports at nearly US$492 million in 2024 and about US$445 million in 2025. The United States and Germany were the largest buyers in TDAP's 2021 data.

What is SIMAP?

SIMAP is the Surgical Instruments Manufacturers Association of Pakistan, the trade body for Sialkot's instrument makers. It offers members services such as trade delegations and document attestation, and runs a searchable members directory.

How can I check the steel quality of Sialkot instruments?

Ask the maker for the steel grade and a material test report. The Sialkot Material Testing Laboratory (SIMTEL), run by TDAP and SIMAP, tests the composition of stainless steel for manufacturers.
