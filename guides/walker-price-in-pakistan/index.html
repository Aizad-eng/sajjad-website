Walker price in Pakistan: standard, wheeled and rollator walkers

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Walker prices in Pakistan depend mainly on the type: a standard folding frame costs least, a two-wheel walker adds wheels and glides, and a rollator with four wheels, brakes and a seat costs most. Check the weight capacity, handle height range, frame weight and ISO 11199 standard before you order.

A walker is a frame the user holds with both hands for support while walking, and families often look for one when someone's balance or strength is reduced. This guide explains the three main kinds, standard frames, wheeled walkers and rollators, the standards that cover them, and what to check on the specification sheet before you order. Which walker suits a person is best decided with their doctor or physiotherapist.

What is a walker and how is it different from a rollator?

A walker is a frame the user holds with both hands for support while walking, and a rollator is a walker with wheels on every leg, hand brakes and usually a seat. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) describes a mechanical walker in 21 CFR 890.3825 as a four-legged device with a metal frame that gives moderate weight support while walking, for people who lack strength, good balance or endurance. The FDA places it in Class I, under general controls.

International standards treat the two as separate products. ISO 11199-1 covers walking frames and ISO 11199-2 covers rollators, which it describes as walking aids with wheels that are manipulated by both arms.

A standard frame is lifted and placed forward with each step. A two-wheel walker can be pushed forward, with the rear glides sliding along the floor. A rollator rolls on all its wheels and is slowed with hand brakes. Drive also lists three-wheel rollators alongside its four-wheel models.

Which standards apply to walkers and rollators?

The main international standards are ISO 11199-1:2021 for walking frames and ISO 11199-2:2021 for rollators. Both set requirements and test methods for safety, ergonomics, performance, and the information the manufacturer must provide, including marking and labelling. Both apply to products made for users of at least 35 kg.

The rollator standard has an amendment, ISO 11199-2:2021/Amd 1:2024, titled "Removal of brake requirements". Rollators with horizontal forearm supports, called walking tables, fall under a separate part, ISO 11199-3. When comparing imported models, ask the supplier which standard the product was tested to and ask for the test report.

How do you choose the right handle height?

The right height is set for the individual user, so choose a walker whose adjustment range covers that height. Height is usually set with push buttons on the legs, and Drive describes push-button mechanisms on its folding walkers. The Drive 10210-1 comes in two ranges, one roughly 81 to 99 cm and a lower one of roughly 64 to 82 cm, while the R726 rollator adjusts from about 81 to 94 cm. A physiotherapist can set the height on the first fitting. Measure the user standing in their usual shoes and compare it with the range on the specification sheet before ordering.

What weight capacity should a walker have?

The rated weight capacity should be above the user's body weight. The Drive examples above are rated 350 lb (about 159 kg) for the folding frames and 300 lb (about 136 kg) for the R726 rollator. Heavier users need a bariatric walker. Drive lists bariatric folding walkers, such as the 10220-1, as a separate range. If the user plans to sit on a rollator, ask the supplier for the rated load of the seat as well.

Is an aluminium or steel walker better?

Neither is better for everyone, so compare the stated frame weight and capacity of each model. Aluminium is common on lightweight folding walkers: the Drive 10200-1 uses 1 inch aluminium tubing and weighs about 2.5 kg. A frame that is lifted with every step should be as light as safely possible for the user. For a rollator, which is pushed rather than lifted, weight matters most when folding it into a car or carrying it up stairs. Drive offers rollators in both aluminium and steel.

What should you check on a rollator before buying?

Check the brakes, seat height, wheel size, width and how it folds. Specific points:

  • Brakes: test that the hand brakes stop the rollator and lock for sitting. Ask the supplier how the brakes are adjusted.
  • Seat height: the R726 seat is 20 inches (about 51 cm) from the floor. The user should be able to sit and stand safely.
  • Wheel size: match the wheels to where the rollator will be used, indoors or outdoors. The R726 has 6 inch casters with soft grip tires.
  • Width: the R726 measures 24.5 by 27.5 inches (about 62 by 70 cm). Measure doorways and bathroom entrances at home.
  • Extras: a backrest, basket or pouch. The R726 has a removable padded backrest and a zipped pouch under the seat.

What accessories can you add to a walker or rollator?

Common add-ons include baskets, trays, cup holders, cane holders, backrests and replacement brakes. Drive's rollator range, for example, lists baskets for four-wheel rollators, a rollator tray, cup holders, cane holders, backrests and spare brakes as separate items. Accessories are usually made for a specific frame, so give the supplier the exact model when ordering spares. For standard walkers, ask whether replacement rubber tips and rear glide caps are available.

What changes walker prices in Pakistan?

The price depends on the type, frame material, brand and accessories. A plain folding frame is the simplest product. A two-wheel walker adds wheels and glides. A rollator adds four wheels, brakes, a seat and often a backrest and basket, so it costs more. Other factors:

  • Bariatric rating: heavy-duty frames for higher weight limits.
  • Brand: branded imports cost more than generic frames.
  • Exchange rate and freight: most walkers are imported, and rollators are bulky to ship.
  • Quantity: hospitals and physiotherapy clinics ordering several units may get a different unit price.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a walker and a rollator?

A walker is a frame that is lifted or pushed, with no wheels or two front wheels, while a rollator has wheels on every leg, hand brakes and usually a seat. They are covered by separate standards, ISO 11199-1 and ISO 11199-2.

How much weight can a walker hold?

It depends on the model. Drive's standard aluminium folding walkers are rated 350 lb (about 159 kg) and its R726 rollator 300 lb (about 136 kg), and bariatric models are made for heavier users.

Are aluminium walkers better than steel walkers?

Neither suits everyone. Aluminium is common on lightweight folding frames, such as Drive's 10200-1 at about 2.5 kg, so compare the stated weight and capacity of each model rather than the material alone.

What height should a walker be?

The height is set for each user, ideally by a physiotherapist, so choose a walker whose adjustment range covers that height. Drive's 10210-1, for example, adjusts from about 81 to 99 cm in its adult range.

Can I sit on a rollator?

Most four-wheel rollators have a seat, such as the 20 inch high seat on the Drive R726. Check that the brakes lock and ask for the seat's rated load before using it as a seat.
