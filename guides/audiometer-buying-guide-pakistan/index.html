Audiometer buying guide for clinics in Pakistan: types, transducers and calibration

Updated November 1, 2026 · 8 min read

Quick answer

Choose an audiometer by the tests you need. Screening units cover quick pure tone air conduction checks, diagnostic units add bone conduction and masking, and clinical units add two channels, speech and high frequency testing. Check the declared IEC 60645-1 type, the transducer models supplied, and a calibration record to ISO 389 reference levels for that exact headset and bone conductor.

An audiometer is only as reliable as the earphones and bone conductor plugged into it, and the calibration that ties them together. This guide explains the main audiometer categories, what the IEC 60645-1 type number on a datasheet tells you, which transducers are common, and what calibration to ISO 389 reference levels means. It ends with the questions to send any supplier before you pay. It covers equipment only. Which tests your patients need is a decision for your audiologist or ENT specialist.

What is the difference between screening, diagnostic and clinical audiometers?

Manufacturers group audiometers by what the instrument can test. The Danish maker Interacoustics is a useful example because its catalogue uses all three labels.

  • Screening audiometer. Built for quick pure tone checks. Interacoustics describes its portable AS608 as suited to schools, industrial settings and primary medical practices where quick hearing screening is needed. It offers pure tone and warble tone stimuli.
  • Diagnostic audiometer. Adds bone conduction, masking and usually speech testing. The Interacoustics AD629 comes with a DD45 headset and a B71 bone conductor. Its specification lists narrow band masking noise, speech audiometry and an optional high frequency range up to 20 kHz.
  • Clinical audiometer. A full two channel instrument for audiology departments. Interacoustics describes its AC40 as a two channel clinical audiometer with high frequency audiometry and a built-in free field amplifier as standard.

Match the category to the work you do. Interacoustics positions its screening units for schools, industry and primary practices, its diagnostic units for testing air and bone conduction thresholds, and its clinical units for a full clinical test battery. Your audiologist or ENT specialist should decide which tests the clinic needs, and the equipment choice follows from that.

What does the IEC 60645-1 type number mean?

IEC 60645-1 is the international standard for audiometers. The current fourth edition was published by IEC in March 2017. It covers equipment for both pure tone and speech audiometry, and it replaced the 2012 edition and the separate speech audiometer standard, IEC 60645-2. Its aim is that pure tone tests from 125 Hz to 16 kHz give essentially the same results on any instrument that complies.

The standard classifies audiometers by the range of test signals they present, their mode of operation or their main intended use. Each type has a set of minimum facilities, listed in Table 1 of the standard. The full text is sold by IEC, so in practice you read the type from the manufacturer's datasheet. The examples below show the pattern: the more an instrument can do, the lower its type number.

The type number is a quick filter. It does not tell you the condition of a particular unit, so you still need the full datasheet and a calibration record for that serial number.

Which transducers can an audiometer use?

A transducer is the part that delivers the test signal to the patient. There are four common kinds.

  • Supra-aural headphones rest on the outer ear. Examples are the Telephonics TDH39 and the RadioEar DD45. RadioEar lists the DD45 as a 10 ohm earphone covering 100 Hz to 8 kHz.
  • Circumaural headphones enclose the ear and block more room noise. Examples are the RadioEar DD65v2 and DD450. RadioEar says the DD450 can be used for high frequency testing up to 20 kHz and complies with IEC 60645-1 up to 16 kHz.
  • Insert earphones deliver sound through a tube and foam tip in the ear canal. Examples are the RadioEar IP30, listed as 10 ohm in the Interacoustics AC40 specification, and the EAR-Tone 3A.
  • Bone conductors sit on the bone behind the ear, held by a headband. The RadioEar B71 and B81 are the usual choices.

For a side by side comparison of headphones, read TDH39 vs DD45 vs DD65. For bone conductors, read B71 vs B81.

What does calibration to ISO 389 reference levels mean?

An audiometer dial reads in decibels hearing level (dB HL). Zero dB HL is meant to match the threshold of hearing of young people with normal hearing. The actual sound pressure needed to reach that point changes with frequency and with the earphone model. The ISO 389 series of standards defines that reference zero.

ISO 389-1:2017 sets the reference zero for pure tone air conduction audiometers. It gives the values as reference equivalent threshold sound pressure levels (RETSPLs) for generic supra-aural earphones measured on an IEC 60318-1 ear simulator, plus model specific tables for named earphones.

In plain terms, calibration means a technician connects the earphone to an ear simulator or coupler, or the bone conductor to an artificial mastoid, measures the real output at each frequency, and adjusts the audiometer so that each dial setting produces the reference level for that exact transducer model. The numbers differ between models. At 1000 Hz on an IEC 60318-1 ear simulator, RadioEar lists a RETSPL of 7.5 dB SPL for the DD45 and 4.5 dB for the DD65v2. That is why the audiometer must be set up for the headset actually connected.

RadioEar's DD45 instructions say to make sure the headset is calibrated to the audiometer it will be used with before use. Calibration needs a trained service engineer with measuring equipment, usually the manufacturer's authorised service partner. Ask the supplier who can do this in Pakistan and what interval the manufacturer recommends.

When a supplier gives you a calibration certificate, check that it names the transducer models and serial numbers actually supplied, and the standard used for each.

Can you change the headphones or bone conductor later?

Often yes, but not as a simple plug swap. Many audiometric transducers share the same plug and a 10 ohm impedance, yet each model has its own reference values. The audiometer has to be set up for the new model and calibrated with it. Manufacturers list which transducers each audiometer supports. Interacoustics, for example, lists TDH39, DD450, DD65v2, IP30 and B81 as optional parts for the AD629. Before buying a replacement, confirm that your audiometer model supports it.

What does DRAP require when you buy an audiometer in Pakistan?

Under the DRAP Medical Devices Rules, 2017, an importer needs an establishment licence, and each device needs enlistment (Class A) or registration (Classes B, C and D) before it is imported for sale. DRAP sets the class through Schedule A of the Rules. Ask the supplier which class applies to the model you are buying and for its enlistment or registration number. Our guide to the DRAP Medical Devices Rules explains what to ask for.

What should you ask a supplier before buying an audiometer?

  1. The exact model, the manufacturer and the declared IEC 60645-1 type, with the full datasheet.
  2. Which transducers are included, by model name: for example DD45 or TDH39, B71 or B81, and whether insert earphones such as the IP30 are included.
  3. A calibration certificate for the serial number, showing the date, the ISO 389 parts used for each transducer and who performed it.
  4. Who can recalibrate and repair the unit in Pakistan, and whether spare cushions, cords and headbands are available.
  5. The importer's DRAP establishment licence and the device's enlistment or registration number.
  6. Whether the unit is new, used or refurbished. DRAP issued Medical Devices (Refurbishment) Regulations in September 2026, so ask how a refurbished unit was handled under them.
  7. Warranty terms in writing, including what happens if the unit fails calibration on arrival.
  8. Software and reporting needs, such as PC software, printing and database compatibility, if your clinic needs them.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a screening and a diagnostic audiometer?

A screening audiometer is built for quick pure tone checks, usually by air conduction only. A diagnostic audiometer adds bone conduction and masking, and often speech testing.

What does Type 1, Type 2 or Type 4 mean on an audiometer datasheet?

It is the type declared under IEC 60645-1, which sets minimum facilities for each type. In manufacturer examples, a clinical audiometer is Type 1, a diagnostic one Type 2 and a screening one Type 4.

What is ISO 389?

ISO 389 is a series of standards that defines the reference zero for audiometer calibration. Different parts cover supra-aural earphones, insert earphones, bone vibrators, circumaural earphones and other conditions.

Can I use any audiometric headphone with my audiometer?

Only models the audiometer supports, and only after it has been set up and calibrated for that model. Each headphone has its own reference values even when the plug and impedance match.
