How to clean a nebulizer and look after it

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

To clean a nebulizer, take the cup apart after every treatment, throw away leftover medicine, wash or rinse the parts and let them dry fully. Disinfect on the schedule in your manual, by boiling or a listed disinfectant. Do not wash the tubing or air filter. Keep them dry and replace them on time.

A nebulizer that is not cleaned properly can stop misting well, and damp parts left in a bag are a place for germs to grow. The good news is that cleaning takes only a few minutes after each treatment. The catch is that makers give different instructions, so the right method depends on your model. This guide sets out what the Omron NE-C801 manual, the Monaghan MC-300 insert, National Jewish Health and the home care company Lincare say, so you can compare them with the leaflet in your own box.

Why does a nebulizer need cleaning after every use?

Cleaning after every use stops leftover medicine drying inside the cup and helps prevent infection. The Omron NE-C801 manual gives both reasons: dried medicine can stop the device nebulizing effectively, and regular washing helps prevent infections.

Monaghan makes the same point for its MC-300 cup. It warns that poor cleaning may affect performance and that a build-up of medicine residue can affect how the aerosol forms. In short, a dirty cup can give a weaker mist, so less medicine may reach the lungs.

How do you clean a nebulizer after each treatment?

Take the cup apart, throw away leftover medicine, wash or rinse the parts and let them dry completely.

  1. Wash your hands.
  2. Switch off and unplug the compressor, then pull off the air tube.
  3. Remove the mouthpiece or mask and separate the cup into its parts.
  4. Throw away any medicine left in the cup. Omron says to use fresh medicine every time.
  5. Wash the parts in warm water with liquid dish soap, as National Jewish Health and Monaghan describe, or rinse them under hot tap water, as the Omron manual says.
  6. Rinse off all soap.
  7. Dry the parts fully before you put them away.

Do not wash the tubing. National Jewish Health says to wash all parts except the tubing and finger valve. Monaghan also allows its MC-300 parts on the top rack of a home dishwasher on a normal cycle, taken out before the drying cycle. Check that your own manual allows this before trying it.

How often should you disinfect a nebulizer?

Disinfect as often as your own manual says, which is usually once a day or every other treatment day. Cleaning removes residue. Disinfecting is an extra step that kills more germs, and you always clean first.

The listed methods are boiling, an alcohol or hydrogen peroxide soak, a respiratory equipment disinfectant, or a vinegar soak, but the times differ between makers.

Some rules that apply whatever method you use:

  • Only boil parts your manual says can be boiled. Omron says not to boil the air tube, air filter, filter cover or masks. National Jewish Health also leaves out the mask, tubing and interrupter.
  • Boil in plenty of water and do not let the pan boil dry.
  • Rinse off chemical solutions after soaking. Omron says not to leave cleaning solution in the parts.
  • Use a fresh vinegar or disinfectant solution each time and throw it away after use.
  • Omron says not to use an autoclave, ethylene oxide gas or low temperature plasma sterilizer on the NE-C801.

How should you dry nebulizer parts?

Let the parts air dry fully on a clean, lint-free cloth before you put them together and store them.

National Jewish Health offers a faster option for compressor models: reattach the cup and tubing and run the compressor so the airflow dries the cup. The Omron manual says not to use a microwave oven, dish dryer or hair dryer to dry parts. Lincare suggests storing the dry parts in a sealed plastic bag, and Omron suggests a dry bag.

How do you look after the tubing?

Keep the tubing dry and replace it regularly, because it is hard to clean inside. National Jewish Health says the tubing is not washed and is replaced every two weeks, because it is difficult to clean and dry properly.

The Omron manual warns never to store the air tube with moisture or medicine inside it, as bacteria can grow. If you see droplets after a treatment, leave the tube on the compressor, switch it on for a short time to blow the moisture out, then switch off. Do not use a tube that is bent, kinked or damaged, and replace it if it is.

When should you change the air filter?

For the Omron NE-C801, change the air filter when it changes colour or after about 60 days of use on average, and at once if water or medicine spills on it.

  • Do not wash the filter. Omron says damp filters can cause blockages.
  • Do not replace it with cotton or any other material.
  • Never run the compressor without a filter in place.
  • Wash the filter cover regularly so it does not block, but do not boil it, and make sure it is dry before fitting a new filter.

Other models use different filters and intervals, so check the part number in your own manual.

How do you clean and store the compressor?

Unplug the compressor and wipe the outside with a soft cloth dampened with water or mild detergent, then dry it at once. Omron says never to put the compressor in water or pour liquid on it, and not to use abrasive cleaners.

For storage, Omron advises a clean, safe place away from humid areas such as a bathroom, away from direct sunlight and not inside a hot car. Do not carry the kit with medicine still in the cup, and do not wrap the power cord around the compressor.

When should you replace the nebulizer cup?

Replace the cup when it is damaged or when it reaches the life stated by its maker. Monaghan says its MC-300 can be used by one person for up to 6 months and should be replaced within that time, and it recommends keeping a spare. It also says to replace the cup immediately if you notice any damage.

Lincare advises replacing disposable parts such as masks, tubing and filters as the manufacturer recommends. Omron asks you not to clean the baffle with a brush or pin, as this can damage it. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to clean my nebulizer after every use?

Yes. Omron, Monaghan and National Jewish Health all say to clean the cup after every treatment, because dried medicine can block the mist and damp parts can grow germs.

Can I boil nebulizer parts?

Only the parts your manual allows. Omron says the NE-C801 cup parts can be boiled for 15 to 20 minutes, but not the air tube, filter, filter cover or masks.

Should I wash the nebulizer tubing?

No. National Jewish Health says not to wash the tubing and to replace it every two weeks. Keep it dry by running the compressor briefly if moisture appears.

How often do I change the nebulizer air filter?

For the Omron NE-C801, change it when it changes colour or after about 60 days of use on average, and straight away if it gets wet. Do not wash and reuse it.

Is vinegar enough to disinfect a nebulizer?

National Jewish Health and Lincare list a 1 to 3 vinegar soak, while Omron and Monaghan list boiling or other disinfectants instead. Use a method your own manual lists and ask your doctor if unsure.
