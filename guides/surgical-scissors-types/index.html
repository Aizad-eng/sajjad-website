Surgical scissors types: Mayo, Metzenbaum, iris and more explained

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

The main surgical scissors types are Mayo scissors for dense tissue and sutures, Metzenbaum scissors for delicate tissue and blunt dissection, iris scissors for fine precise cutting, stitch removal scissors for taking out sutures and dressing scissors for bandages. Choose by the material being cut, length, straight or curved blades, tip shape and whether you want tungsten carbide edges.

Surgical scissors come in many patterns that look alike in a catalogue. This guide explains the common surgical scissors types, what straight, curved, sharp and blunt mean, what gold and black handles tell you, and what to write when you order. It covers equipment only. Clinical technique belongs to training and local protocols.

What are Mayo scissors used for?

Mayo scissors are heavy-bladed scissors used mainly to cut dense connective tissue such as fascia, and also sutures, mesh, drains and dressings. They take their name from the Mayo Clinic, where the design was developed.

  • Straight Mayo: cuts tissue near the surface of a wound. Because it is also used to cut sutures, it is often called suture scissors.
  • Curved Mayo: reaches deeper into a wound and is used for thick tissue such as muscle. It can also be used for blunt dissection, where the closed tips are placed in tissue and then opened to spread it.

Mayo scissors are made from stainless steel or titanium, with stainless steel being much cheaper. Common lengths run from about 150 mm (6 inches) to 170 mm (6.75 inches), and extra-long versions are also made.

What are Metzenbaum scissors used for?

Metzenbaum scissors are lighter scissors for cutting delicate tissue and for blunt dissection. They are named after the American surgeon Myron Firth Metzenbaum (1876 to 1944) and are often shortened to Metz.

They have a relatively long shank compared with the blade length. The blades are usually curved, although straight versions exist, and the tips are almost always rounded. The rounded tips let the user separate tissue without cutting it by accident. Some models have tungsten carbide inserts on the cutting edges.

Do not use Metzenbaum scissors to cut sutures or dressings. WHO advises against using fine scissors designed for tissue on suture or other non-tissue material, and recommends larger, blunter scissors for that job.

What are iris scissors and stitch scissors?

Iris scissors are very small scissors with extremely fine, sharp tips, first developed for eye surgery and now used for precise cutting. They come with straight or curved blades.

Single-use minor operations packs from UK suppliers list straight iris stitch scissors of 11.5 cm. Stitch removal scissors are a separate pattern: a University of Bristol clinical skills sheet describes them as having a fine tip and blades designed to make suture removal easier. If your clinic removes many sutures, consider ordering dedicated stitch removal scissors. Our minor surgery instrument set guide shows where iris scissors fit in a typical set.

Which scissors are used for dressings and bandages?

Dressing or bandage scissors are used to cut dressings and bandaging material, and they should be kept away from surgical use. The University of Bristol sheet says plainly that dressing scissors are for dressings and bandages and should not be used in surgery.

Keeping separate dressing scissors means your tissue scissors are not used on dressings. Our dressing set guide lists the other instruments used for wound dressing.

What do straight, curved, sharp and blunt tips mean?

Straight blades are for cutting at or near the surface, while curved blades let the user reach deeper tissue. Tip shape decides how safely the scissors can be pushed into tissue.

  • Sharp/sharp: both tips pointed, for fine, precise cuts. Iris scissors are an example.
  • Blunt/blunt: both tips rounded, so the scissors can be used to separate tissue with less risk of cutting it by accident. Metzenbaum scissors and the Kilner scissors in the table are examples.
  • Sharp/blunt: one pointed and one rounded tip.

When you order, write both the blade shape and the tip shape, for example "Metzenbaum, curved, blunt/blunt, 18 cm".

What do gold and black handles mean on surgical scissors?

Gold handle rings usually mean the scissors have tungsten carbide inserts on the cutting edges. World Precision Instruments describes a common colour code for ring scissors:

  • Gold rings: tungsten carbide inserts.
  • Black rings: SuperCut scissors with one serrated blade.
  • One gold and one black ring: serrations on one blade and tungsten carbide on the other.
  • Blue instruments: usually titanium.

Delasco notes that tungsten carbide inserts help keep scissors sharper for longer. Surgical Holdings explains that the carbide is brazed into the jaw, making the scissors harder and more durable. Treat colour as a convention, not a guarantee, and confirm the insert type in the product description.

Which standard applies to surgical scissors?

ISO 7741 is the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) standard for scissors and shears used in surgery. It sets general requirements and test methods for the materials of component parts, heat treatment and hardness, corrosion resistance, workmanship and cutting ability.

The edition listed by the Estonian standards body EVS at the time of writing (November 2026) is ISO 7741:1986, with no newer version shown. ISO 7153-1:2016 separately lists the metals commonly used to make standard surgical instruments. Ask your supplier which standards and steel grade apply to the scissors you are buying.

How do you choose and look after surgical scissors?

Choose scissors by the material they will cut, the depth of the work and how often they will be used, then protect them with good handling and careful sharpening.

  • Match the job: fine scissors for tissue, heavier scissors for sutures and dressings, as WHO advises.
  • Choose the shortest length that reaches: WHO recommends the shortest instrument that will comfortably reach the operative site.
  • Plan for left-handed users: WHO explains that scissors are made so the blades come together in the right hand. Used in the left hand, the same motion pushes the blades apart unless the user changes their grip.
  • Care for carbide: Surgical Holdings advises checking for cracks around the brazing and sharpening tungsten carbide scissors carefully, because overheating can crack the inserts.
  • Test on arrival: WHO warns that scissors that do not cut easily are frustrating and dangerous. Open and close each pair along the full blade before putting it into service.

Scissors also appear inside some needle holders. Olsen-Hegar and Gillies patterns combine a needle holder and scissors in one instrument; see our needle holder types guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Mayo and Metzenbaum scissors?

Mayo scissors are heavy scissors for dense tissue, sutures and dressings. Metzenbaum scissors are lighter, with long handles, short blades and blunt tips, for delicate tissue and blunt dissection.

Why are some surgical scissors called suture scissors?

Straight Mayo scissors are often called suture scissors because they are used to cut sutures as well as tissue near the wound surface. Fine tissue scissors should not be used for this job.

What are iris scissors used for?

Iris scissors are small scissors with very fine, sharp tips, first designed for eye surgery and now used for precise cutting. Straight iris stitch scissors are a common item in minor operations packs.

What do gold handles on surgical scissors mean?

Gold handle rings usually indicate tungsten carbide inserts on the cutting edges, which help the scissors stay sharp for longer. Confirm the insert type in the product description.

Which ISO standard covers surgical scissors?

ISO 7741 covers scissors and shears used in surgery, including materials, hardness, corrosion resistance, workmanship and cutting ability. The edition listed at the time of writing is ISO 7741:1986.
