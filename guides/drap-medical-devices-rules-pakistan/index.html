DRAP Medical Devices Rules 2017: what they mean for buyers in Pakistan

Updated November 1, 2026 · 9 min read

Quick answer

Under the DRAP Medical Devices Rules, 2017, devices fall into risk Classes A to D. A business needs a DRAP establishment licence to import or manufacture them, and each product must be enlisted (Class A) or registered (Classes B to D). Before buying, ask the supplier for the licence, the product's enlistment or registration number and a Form-5 warranty.

If you buy medical equipment or disposables in Pakistan, the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) Medical Devices Rules, 2017 decide who may import and sell them. This guide explains the parts that matter to a buyer: risk classes, establishment licences, enlistment and registration, import rules and the documents to ask a supplier for. Everything here reflects the Rules and DRAP web pages at the time of writing (November 2026). Rules change, so check the current text on dra.gov.pk before relying on any point. This is a plain English summary, not legal advice.

What are the DRAP Medical Devices Rules, 2017?

DRAP notified the Medical Devices Rules, 2017 on 16 January 2018 through S.R.O. 32(I)/2018, under section 23 of the DRAP Act, 2012. DRAP publishes a consolidated copy as amended till April 2022, and it has issued further amendments since. The Rules are administered by the Medical Device Board (MDB).

The Rules cover any instrument, apparatus, machine, appliance, implant, in vitro reagent, software or material intended for purposes such as diagnosis, prevention, monitoring or treatment of disease or injury, supporting life, control of conception or disinfection of medical devices. A product whose main action is pharmacological, immunological or metabolic is a drug, not a device. In practice this covers everything from syringes and gloves to audiometers and ventilators.

How does DRAP classify medical devices into Class A to D?

Schedule A of the Rules sets four risk classes, with Class A the lowest hazard and Class D the highest. The class depends on the device's potential to harm a patient or user, which follows from its intended use and the technology it uses. Where two rules apply, the higher class wins. DRAP's own examples are below.

ALowTongue depressors, disposable masksEnlistment
BLow to moderateHypodermic needles, suction equipmentRegistration
CModerate to highLung ventilator, bone fixation plateRegistration
DHighHeart valves, implantable defibrillatorRegistration

If there is a dispute over the class of a device, the establishment can ask the MDB in writing to decide. As a buyer, you do not need to classify devices yourself. Ask the supplier which class DRAP has given the product.

What is an establishment licence?

An establishment licence is DRAP's permission for a business to manufacture or import medical devices. The Rules set two types: a licence to manufacture and a licence to import. Each premises needs its own licence, and the licence is valid for five years unless suspended or cancelled.

To get an import licence, the Rules require the applicant to have suitable premises with enough storage space, to follow Good Distribution Practices for Medical Devices, and to run import, storage, distribution and sale under the supervision of at least one full time qualified person. The qualifications listed include degrees in pharmacy, biomedical engineering, microbiology, biotechnology, biochemistry and medical physics. DRAP's application process page says applications go through its online system at e.dra.gov.pk with a hard copy on Form-2, and that a DRAP panel may visit the storage premises.

A licensed importer must also issue a warranty on Form-5 for devices it sells, keep complete batch records of imports and sales, take responsibility for the quality, safety and performance of what it imports through the supply chain, and distribute only to licensed retail sellers.

What is the difference between enlistment and registration?

Both put a specific product on DRAP's medical device register. Rule 13 says Class A devices are enlisted and Class B, C and D devices are registered. Only a licensed manufacturer, or a licensed importer acting as the authorised representative of the foreign maker, can apply. Enlistment and registration are each valid for five years and must be renewed.

The holder must print the approved labelling details, including licence number, registration number and maximum retail price, on the product labelling. A certificate of enlistment or registration cannot be transferred to another person without MDB approval.

What do the Rules say about importing medical devices?

Rule 21 is short and clear: a medical device may be imported only if the importer holds a valid establishment licence, the device is enlisted or registered, and the importer has premises for proper storage. Rule 26 adds that no medical device may be released from customs until the importer has a clearance certificate from an officer authorised by DRAP.

There are narrow exceptions. Rule 22 allows small quantities for clinical investigation, examination, test or analysis under an import permit. Rule 24 allows personal use imports, covered below. Rule 24A, added in 2022, covers donations, with extra approval needed for Class C and D devices.

Can a hospital or an individual import a device that is not registered?

Only in limited cases under Rule 24, and you should confirm the current procedure with DRAP first.

  • Personal use. Small quantities may come in a passenger's own baggage for personal use. If not in baggage, the MDB or an authorised officer must give permission before import.
  • Hospital patients. A public or private hospital may import a device that is not enlisted or registered with prior approval from the MDB or an authorised officer. Conditions include: the device is not sold in the market, it is on free sale in its country of origin, it is used only in that hospital, it is not enlisted, registered or available in Pakistan, and a DRAP clearance certificate is obtained before customs clearance.

These routes are not a way to stock devices for sale. For commercial supply, the licensed importer route in Rule 21 applies. Our guide on importing medical equipment from China covers the practical side.

What has changed recently?

  • April 2022. S.R.O. 559(I)/2022 amended the Rules, including conditions for small quantity import permits and the new donation rule.
  • June 2025. S.R.O. 1049(I)/2025 amended Rule 19 so that in war or national emergency, registration holders must prioritise supply to civil and armed forces hospitals.
  • September 2026. DRAP notified the Medical Devices (Refurbishment) Regulations, 2026 under S.R.O. 1450(I)/2026. Refurbished devices are enlisted or registered through a licensed manufacturer or a licensed importer acting as authorised representative, and single use, implantable and disposable devices must not be refurbished.

Application fees are set under rule 63 of the Rules. We do not quote them here. Check the current fee schedule with DRAP.

What should a clinic ask a supplier for?

Before you buy, ask for the following and keep copies on file.

  1. A copy of the importer's DRAP establishment licence, with its number and expiry date. If the seller is a distributor, ask which licensed importer brought the product in.
  2. The enlistment (Class A) or registration (Class B, C or D) number for the exact product and model.
  3. A warranty on Form-5 with the invoice, which the Rules require licensed importers to issue.
  4. Labelling that shows the importer's licence details, the registration number and the maximum retail price, as the licence conditions require.
  5. For used or refurbished equipment, how it was handled under the 2026 Refurbishment Regulations.
  6. The manufacturer's own documents, such as the instructions for use and any certificates from the country of origin.

How can you check a supplier or product yourself?

DRAP publishes lists on its website of licensed manufacturers and importers, enlisted Class A devices and registered Class B, C and D devices. When we checked, the page had last been updated in September 2025, and DRAP says the lists are provisional and may contain errors or omissions. Use them as a first check and ask the supplier for the certificate if a product is missing.

Frequently asked questions

What are the four DRAP medical device classes?

Class A is low hazard, Class B low to moderate, Class C moderate to high and Class D high hazard. DRAP gives tongue depressors as a Class A example and heart valves as Class D.

What is the difference between enlistment and registration with DRAP?

Class A devices are enlisted and Class B, C and D devices are registered. Both are valid for five years and can only be applied for by a licensed manufacturer or importer.

How long is a DRAP establishment licence valid?

The Rules say five years from issue, unless it is suspended or cancelled earlier. It must then be renewed.

Can a hospital import a device that is not registered in Pakistan?

Rule 24 allows it with prior MDB approval under conditions, including that the device is not sold, is on free sale in its country of origin and is not available in Pakistan. Confirm the current procedure with DRAP.

How can I check whether a product is registered with DRAP?

DRAP publishes provisional lists of enlisted and registered devices and licensed importers on its website. If a product is missing, ask the supplier for the certificate.
