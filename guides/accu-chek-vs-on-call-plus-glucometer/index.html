Accu-Chek vs On Call Plus glucometer: how to compare them

Updated November 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Quick answer

Accu-Chek Instant and Active meters need no coding and store 500 to 720 results, while the On Call Plus from ACON Laboratories uses a code chip with each strip box, needs a 1 microlitre sample and gives results in 10 seconds. Compare accuracy evidence, strip supply and running cost, not only the meter.

Accu-Chek and On Call Plus, made by ACON Laboratories, are both common glucometer names in Pakistan. They work in different ways and sit at different points in their makers' ranges. This guide compares the Accu-Chek Instant and Accu-Chek Active, which appear on Accu-Chek's Pakistan website, with the On Call Plus, using the manufacturers' own published specifications.

What is the main difference between Accu-Chek and On Call Plus?

The main difference is in coding, speed and sample size. Accu-Chek's Pakistan pages list automatic coding for the Instant and no coding for the Active. The On Call Plus manual says to use the code chip that comes with each box of strips. The On Call Plus manual also gives a 10 second test time and a 1 microlitre minimum sample, against less than 4 seconds and 0.6 microlitres for the Accu-Chek Instant.

The On Call Plus manual gives a measurement range of 20 to 600 mg/dL. Accu-Chek's Pakistan pages do not list a measurement range, so check the meter insert for that figure.

What do ISO 15197 results say about each meter?

ISO 15197 is the accuracy standard that independent databases use to rate blood glucose meters, and it has a 2003 and a 2013 version. Medaval treats ISO 15197:2015 as the same as the 2013 version. The Medaval device database lists the On Call Plus as passing ISO 15197:2003 and failing ISO 15197:2013 in a general population. Medaval links this to a 2017 multicentre study of 19 meters made in the Asia-Pacific region.

ACON's own On Call brand page did not list the On Call Plus at the time of writing (November 2026). Its current international range is the On Call Extra family, and ACON states the On Call Extra meter is compliant with ISO 15197:2015. The Accu-Chek Pakistan product pages we opened did not state an ISO 15197 result, so ask for the strip insert or check an independent database before you decide. Our guide on glucometer accuracy and ISO 15197 explains the standard.

Why do test strips matter more than the meter?

Strips are the ongoing cost, and each meter only accepts its own strips. The On Call Plus manual says to use only On Call Plus strips with the On Call Plus meter. Accu-Chek lists separate Instant and Active strips on its Pakistan site. Before buying a meter, check that its strips are easy to get in your city and look at the expiry dates on the stock offered. See our guide on glucometer strips in Pakistan for what affects strip cost.

What do sample size and haematocrit range mean in daily use?

Sample size is the smallest drop of blood the strip needs. A smaller figure can mean fewer repeat finger pricks when the drop is small. Haematocrit is the share of red cells in the blood, and each meter is only specified within a range. The Accu-Chek Instant lists 10-65%, the Active 20-55% and the On Call Plus 30-55%. If a user's haematocrit may fall outside a meter's range, ask the doctor which meter is suitable.

Why does the On Call Plus need a code chip?

The code chip tells the meter how to read that batch of strips. The On Call Plus manual says a new code chip comes with every box and must be inserted into the meter. If an old chip is left in, the meter may read the new strips with the wrong settings. Accu-Chek's Instant and Active pages list automatic coding or no coding, which removes that step. ACON's newer On Call Extra meter is also listed with no coding.

Which features matter for a clinic and which for home use?

For home use, buyers often look at ease of use, display size and phone connection. The Accu-Chek Instant has a colour-coded target range indicator, a backlit display, a strip ejector and Bluetooth to the mySugr app. The Accu-Chek Active lets blood be applied with the strip in or out of the meter, lets you mark results with symbols for particular situations, and has a visual check area on the strip vial. Its meter also detects when the blood drop is too small, and more blood can then be applied. For a clinic testing many people, buyers usually also look at memory size, control solution and strip supply. Testing frequency and targets are medical decisions, so follow your doctor's advice.

Should you look at the Accu-Chek Instant S or On Call Extra instead?

Both makers sell other meters, and the names are easy to mix up. Accu-Chek's Pakistan site lists three meters at the time of writing (November 2026): the Accu-Chek Active, the Accu-Chek Instant and the Accu-Chek Instant S, and it offers side-by-side comparison pages for them. It lists Active, Instant and Performa test strips.

ACON lists the On Call Extra family for international markets, not the United States. ACON gives these figures for the Extra range:

What should you check before buying either glucometer?

  1. The exact meter name, as names like Plus, Extra, Instant and Instant S are different products.
  2. The ISO 15197 version the meter meets, from the insert or an independent database.
  3. Strip availability and expiry dates where you live.
  4. Whether coding is automatic or needs a chip.
  5. The units shown, mg/dL or mmol/L, as some meters cannot be switched.
  6. Warranty terms and who handles them in Pakistan.

Frequently asked questions

Does the On Call Plus need coding?

Yes. The On Call Plus manual says to insert the code chip that comes with each new box of test strips.

Does the Accu-Chek Instant need coding?

No. Accu-Chek's Pakistan page lists automatic coding for the Accu-Chek Instant.

Does the On Call Plus meet ISO 15197:2013?

Medaval lists the On Call Plus as passing ISO 15197:2003 and failing ISO 15197:2013 in a general population. ACON states its newer On Call Extra meter is compliant with ISO 15197:2015.

Can I use On Call Plus strips in an Accu-Chek meter?

No. Each meter only works with its own strips, and the On Call Plus manual says to use only On Call Plus strips.

How fast are Accu-Chek and On Call Plus results?

Accu-Chek lists under 4 seconds for the Instant and about 5 seconds for the Active. The On Call Plus manual lists 10 seconds.
